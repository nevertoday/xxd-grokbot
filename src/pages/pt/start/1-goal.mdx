The first time a lot of people open Grok Bot, they start with "send this email for me" or "fill in this form for me." It will, of course. But once it's done, next time you have to ask again and keep an eye on it again. Used like that, it's just an errand runner.

The author, poteto, builds Grok Bot at SpaceX. At the start of a [long article](https://x.com/poteto/status/2123159537016533493), the author makes a plain point: automation is really a way of thinking, and anyone can learn it. It has just two parts, a goal and a process.

## An apple pie says it all

Think about baking an apple pie. What you want is the pie, and that's the goal. Buying apples, making the dough, putting it together, baking it and letting it cool are the process. Changing a flat tire works the same way. What you want is a wheel you can drive on again. Jacking up the car, taking the old tire off and putting the new one on are only the process.

The goal is what you want at the end, and the process is how you get there. Automation means handing the process to a machine, so all you ask for is the result.

Grok Bot can take a lot of process off your hands, because a <Term k="bot" /> there has a computer in the cloud. It can click around, open apps and look things up online, so most of what you can do on a computer, it can do too. That leaves you just one thing to think through:

<mark><strong>Before you hand anything to a bot, get clear on the result you want.</strong></mark>

## Why "send an email" isn't enough

Because sending an email is only one step in the process. The author says the most common mistake is taking the process for the goal.

If all you say is "send an email," all the bot does is send an email. But tell it what the email is actually for, say, getting every new customer's booking scheduled, and it can work from that goal: where the information comes from, what counts as done, which steps it could skip. The author also says not to get too attached to the way you do it now. The bot may find an easier way.

## Now write your sentence

Get a sheet of paper, or open the notes app on your phone. Think of something you do on a computer every few days. In one sentence, write the result you really want, then add the other half: what it's for.

For the weekly meeting slides, you could write:

> I want everyone to have slides that show this week's progress before Monday's meeting, so that we spend less of the meeting reading numbers aloud and more of it making decisions.

Read it back. If your sentence is about an action like fill in, send, copy or click, it's still just a step. Ask yourself what doing that is for, and put the answer in. If you can't think of the second half, think about what the job does for your work, whether it brings in money or saves money. Or just ask a colleague.

If no job comes to mind, the author gives these examples in the article. Pick the one most like yours: saving toward a car or a house, booking customers for a small business, giving customers an experience good enough that they leave good reviews, hiring for a small business, collecting feedback on a product, preparing slides for a weekly meeting, building new features for a product.

## Two things you'll need for the next lesson

A computer. Mac, Windows and Linux all work, and you install the Grok Bot desktop app on it first. And a plan that includes Grok Bot. On The X Subscription, Premium, Super and Ultra include it, and Plus doesn't. For details, see the official [Grok Bot on The X Subscription](https://docs.x.ai/x-subscription/grok-bot).

<Callout type="tip" title="Three lessons are enough">
Once you have this sentence, the rest follows. In lesson 2 you install Grok Bot, make a bot and hand it this sentence. In lesson 3 you tell it how you usually do the job and let it do it once, and when it gets it right, you have it do the job on a schedule by itself. After these three lessons you can put it to work. Everything else on the site is there to look up when you need it.
</Callout>

## If something's off

<Q q="I've thought about it, and I can't come up with a goal.">

Then leave it blank for now and go straight to lesson 2. Once your bot exists, you can have it interview you, one question at a time, until the goal is clear. The message for that is in lesson 2, under "[If something's off](/pt/start/2-first-bot/#interview)."

</Q>
<Q q="I have several jobs I want to hand off.">

Pick one first. A bot looks after one job, so everything it remembers is about that job, and you only explain it once. Give each of the other jobs its own bot later.

</Q>
<Q q="I have Grok on my phone. Is that it?">

No. Grok is an app for chatting. Grok Bot is a different app that you install separately, and lesson 2 walks you through it.

</Q>

If you're still unsure, see the official [pricing answer](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/faq#how-much-does-grok-bot-cost).

</Q>

These three lessons use the desktop app on a computer. The phone app (iPhone or Android) goes alongside it. Sign in there with the same account, and you can keep chatting with the same bots and approve what they want to do.

</Q>
