You've done this job many times. The steps live in your head, so familiar you don't think about them. But Potato doesn't know them. In lesson 1, dinner on the table was the goal, and shopping, washing, chopping and cooking were the process. The way you do this job is your recipe, and this lesson is about telling it to Potato.

You don't have to get it right the first time. The author's approach is to have it restate things first: you talk, it says the goal and the process back to you in its own words, and you fix what's off, a few rounds back and forth. Only when the restatement is right does it start. So this lesson goes in this order:

<mark><strong>First get it to restate your process right, then let it do the job once, and when that's right, have it run on a schedule by itself.</strong></mark>

This whole lesson happens in your chat with Potato, in five steps. The first three are only about the process, and it doesn't start work yet.

## Step 1: Think through how you do it now

Don't start typing yet. Picture yourself doing the job. The author lists these things, and you can talk about all of them:

- The steps you usually take, what comes first and what comes next
- What you watch out for, where mistakes happen and what you always double-check
- The apps and tools you use
- How you check it was done right, what you look at again when you're done
- Where the information comes from, like email, your team chat, or an app or website
- Where feedback comes from, like customer reviews or what your colleagues tell you

You don't need to write anything down. In the next step you just talk. Take the parent from lesson 1. You might say something like:

> School and the after-school activities send their notices to my email, a dozen or so a week. I usually go through them on Sunday evening. Anything with a date, like a parent-teacher meeting, a payment or something to bring, goes on my calendar, and I add a note about what to bring. What I miss most is the one line buried in the middle of a long email, like "Please bring an old T-shirt on Thursday." For payments, I check the amount and then pay it myself. Sometimes the teacher just writes "next Wednesday," and I have to work out the date. Usually I only find out I missed something when my kid comes home and says everyone else had it.

If you're not a parent, it works the same way. Just talk about your own job. The office worker from lesson 1 might say:

> I usually start the weekly report around three on Friday. I go back through the week's chat and the docs I've changed, pick out what got done, and write it up in last week's format. What I'm most afraid of missing is a favor I did for another team, because that's usually just a line or two in the chat. I check the numbers against the project sheet, and once they match, it goes out.

The student might say:

> Every night before bed I click through the sites for my three classes to see if there's new homework. When a teacher moves a due date, they usually say so in an email, and the site still shows the old date. That's the one I miss.

## Step 2: Say it all in one go

Talking is faster than typing, and the author suggests voice input too. Say whatever comes to mind, in any order.

1. Copy the message below and paste it into the message box. Don't send it yet.
2. Delete the part in square brackets and leave the cursor there.
3. Hold `Cmd+D` (`Ctrl+D` on Windows and Linux) while you talk, and let go when you're done. That shortcut is **Start voice input** in the message box.
4. Read the text it typed, fix any words it misheard, then send.

<Copy id="process" title="Copy, paste, and replace the brackets with what you say" code={`Potato, here's how I usually do this myself:
[Say your process out loud]
Think about how you could take the whole thing off my hands.
Don't start yet. First tell me in plain words what you think my goal and my process are, so I can check and correct you. Let's get the process settled first.`} />

## Step 3: Read its restatement and correct it a few times

<Reply title="Illustration: roughly what its reply looks like" code={`Let me restate it so you can check. I haven't started anything.

Goal
Nothing with a date in a school or activity notice gets missed,
so your kid never gets to school without what they need.

Process
1. Every Sunday evening, read this week's school and activity notices in your email.
2. Read long emails all the way through, so a line buried in the middle doesn't slip by.
3. Anything with a date, like a parent-teacher meeting, a payment or something to bring, goes on your calendar with a note about what to bring.
4. For payments, I only tell you the amount and the due date. I don't pay.
5. Remind you the day before each one.

One question: when the teacher just writes "next Wednesday," should I count from the day the email was sent?`} />

First look at the goal. Does it match the lines you wrote in lesson 1? Then look at the steps. If something is missing or wrong, just type it in, like "In step 3, also note which kid it's for." And answer its questions the way things really are.

The first pass doesn't have to be perfect. See how it restates things, fill in what it doesn't know yet, and go back and forth a few times, until you read it and think "yes, that's how I do it." At this point it should still be waiting for your OK, and the chat should show no sign of it opening websites or using apps.

## Step 4: Let it do the job once

When the restatement is right, send this:

<Copy id="go" title="Send this once the restatement is right" code={`Yes, go with that, and start with this week.
Connect whatever apps you need.
When you're done, show me first. Don't send anything to anyone.`} />

Once it starts, it stops now and then to ask you something. Two kinds of card are the most common:

- One asks you to sign in to an app. When it needs an app, like your email, it asks you to sign in. Follow the card and finish signing in in your browser. When you see **Authorize** or **Authenticate**, choose it. An app you connect this way is a <Term k="连接的应用" />.
- One asks for your OK. The card says what it wants to do, and this is an <Term k="审批" />. If you understand it, choose **Allow once**.

When it needs a website password, it shows a **Secure Form**. Type the password there, where the bot never sees it. <u>Never type a password into the chat.</u>

<Callout type="ok">
Cards appear in the chat, and after you answer, it carries on. At the end it sends you a message saying what it made and where it put it.
</Callout>

Open what it made and look it over yourself. If anything is wrong, tell it, and let it fix it.

## Step 5: Once it's right, have it run on a schedule

First tell it what to fix and what to keep, like how each calendar entry should read. When the result is right, send this:

<Copy id="routine" title="Change the time and the job to yours, then send" code={`You got it right this time. From now on, every Sunday at 8:00 PM, go through this week's notices the same way, put them on my calendar, then tell me here what you added.
For payments, just tell me. Don't pay. If a date's unclear, ask me here instead of guessing.`} />

That one is the parent's. If what you're handing off is a weekly report or homework, you might change it like this:

- Weekly report: "From now on, every Friday at 12:00 PM, draft this week's report the same way and show it to me here. Don't send it to anyone."
- Homework: "From now on, every Monday at 7:00 AM, go through my three class sites and my school email, then tell me here what's due on which day this week. Flag any due date that moved."

It sets this up as a <Term k="routine" />, which means that when the time comes, it starts by itself, without you asking. It runs even with your laptop closed, because it works on the computer in the cloud.

Check that it's set up:

1. Click the bot's name at the top of the chat to open its details.
2. On the **Details** tab, find the routine and open it.
3. Check that **When to run** shows the right time.

<Callout type="ok">
**Details** has a row with the routine's name and when it runs. Inside the routine, **When to run** describes the time you chose in words.
</Callout>

A new routine doesn't run right away. It waits for its next scheduled time. To try it now, choose **Test** in the routine, though some routines don't show it. A test run really does the work.

<Callout type="warn" title="Every run uses some of your plan">
Each time a routine runs, it uses some of your plan's usage. So start with something like once a week. An hourly schedule can use a week of usage in one day.
</Callout>

## You know how now

Every job you hand off from now on goes the same way. Write a few plain lines about what bugs you and what you want. Make a bot just for that job and hand it those lines. Tell it how you do the job and have it restate it first. Then let it get the job right once before you have it run on a schedule.

## If something's off

<Q q="It started working before I confirmed.">

Send "Stop for now." Then tell it: "First tell me the goal and the process as you understand them. Don't start until I say OK."

</Q>
<Q q="I held Cmd+D and talked, but no text appeared.">

If your computer asks whether Grok Bot can use the microphone, allow it. If Grok Bot says microphone access was denied, allow it in your system settings. If talking still doesn't work for you, that's fine. Just type.

</Q>
<Q q="I don't know what I've left out.">

Let it ask you:

<Copy id="ask-me" code={`I think there's stuff about this job I haven't told you yet. Ask me about it: how I make decisions, where my information comes from, how I check the work, and when you should come to me. One question at a time. Then walk me through a few real examples, and I'll tell you right there what's off.`} />

</Q>
<Q q="I don't understand a card, and I'm not sure whether to approve it.">

Reply first: "I don't get this card. Tell me in plain words what you want to do, or show me a draft first." If you're still unsure, choose **Deny**, and it won't do it.

</Q>
<Q q="It asks me about the same kind of card every time.">

If you trust that action, choose **Always allow**, and from then on it does similar actions on its own without the card. Actions that match an **Ask first** rule you set still get a card, though.

</Q>
<Q q="It seems stuck, and nothing has happened for a while.">

Scroll up in the chat and look for a question, a card or a sign-in waiting for you. If there's none, send a message telling it what to do next. To make it stop, send "Stop for now."

</Q>
<Q q="The time came, and it didn't start by itself.">

Open the routine and check three things:

- It doesn't say **Paused**.
- **When to run** shows the right time. The time zone is in **Settings**, under **Bot**, labeled **Timezone**.
- A new routine waits for its next time. To try it now, choose **Test**.

</Q>
<Q q="It says I've used up my usage.">

It isn't broken. Your usage for this week is used up, and it resets every week. To keep going now, see the official [usage limit is reached](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/troubleshooting#your-usage-limit-is-reached).

</Q>
