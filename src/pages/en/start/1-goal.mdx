The first time a lot of people open Grok Bot, they start with "send this email for me" or "fill in this form for me." It will, of course. But once it's done, next time you have to ask again and keep an eye on it again. Used like that, it's just an errand runner.

The author, poteto, builds Grok Bot at SpaceX. At the start of a [long article](https://x.com/poteto/status/2123159537016533493), the author makes a plain point: automation is really a way of thinking, and anyone can learn it. It has just two parts, a goal and a process.

## Think about dinner

Take dinner on a weeknight. What you want is a meal on the table, and that's the goal. Shopping, washing, chopping and cooking are the process. Paying the electricity bill works the same way. What you want is for the lights to stay on with no late fee. Remembering the date, logging in, finding the bill and paying it are only the process.

The goal is what you want at the end, and the process is how you get there. Automation means handing the process to a machine, so all you ask for is the result.

Grok Bot can take a lot of process off your hands, because a <Term k="bot" /> there has a computer in the cloud. It can click around, open apps and look things up online, so most of what you can do on a computer, it can do too, like logging in to a website and finding a bill. That leaves you just one thing to think through:

<mark><strong>Before you hand anything to a bot, get clear on the result you want.</strong></mark>

## Why "send an email" isn't enough

Because sending an email is only one step in the process. The author says the most common mistake is taking the process for the goal.

If all you say is "send an email," all the bot does is send an email. But tell it what the email is actually for, say, getting paid on time for work you've finished, and it can work from that goal: which jobs haven't been paid yet, when to send a reminder, whether the invoice should have gone out already, which steps it could skip. The author also says not to get too attached to the way you do it now. The bot may find an easier way. Think of that dinner again: on a tired night you order in, and dinner still ends up on the table.

## Now write your lines

Get a sheet of paper, or open the notes app on your phone. Think of something you do on a computer every few days. Then write two or three sentences, the way you'd vent to a friend who's good at this stuff. Say what bugs you about it, then the result you actually want, and let the reason come out naturally, with "so …" or "that way …".

If you're not sure how to start, here's how a few different people might put it. Pick the one most like you and make it about your own job:

- A student: "My due dates are split between school emails and the course website, and I always remember the night before. Every morning, tell me what I still haven't handed in and when it's due, so I'm not up all night again."
- Someone with an office job: "Every Friday afternoon I'm digging through chat and docs to piece together my weekly report, and I hate it. Draft this week's in the same format as last week's, and I'll tweak it and hand it in. That way my Friday afternoon is free."
- A parent: "School and after-school activities send so many notices that I always miss something: a parents' meeting, a payment, something my kid needs to bring. Whenever a notice mentions a date, put it on my calendar and remind me the day before, so my kid doesn't get to school and find out they don't have it."
- A freelancer: "I finish a job and forget to send the invoice, and then the money takes a month or two to come in. When a job's done, draft the invoice and the email for me to check, and tell me if it isn't paid by the due date, so I'm not keeping track of who owes me in my head."
- Someone who runs a hair salon: "Clients book by email or on my booking page, and I keep it all in my head. Last week two of them showed up at the same time. Put every booking in one list and check with me when two clash, so nobody comes in and has to wait."

Read yours back. If what you're asking for is a single action like fill in, send, copy or click, it's still just one step of the process. Ask yourself what doing it is for, and add the answer. If nothing comes to mind, think about what you'd get back once it's done: time, money, or one less thing on your mind. Or ask a colleague, or someone at home.

## Two things you'll need for the next lesson

A computer. Mac, Windows and Linux all work, and you install the Grok Bot desktop app on it first. And a plan that includes Grok Bot. On The X Subscription, Premium, Super and Ultra include it, and Plus doesn't. For details, see the official [Grok Bot on The X Subscription](https://docs.x.ai/x-subscription/grok-bot).

<Callout type="tip" title="Three lessons are enough">
Once you have these lines, the rest follows. In lesson 2 you install Grok Bot, make a bot and hand it what you wrote. In lesson 3 you tell it how you usually do the job and let it do it once, and when it gets it right, you have it do the job on a schedule by itself. After these three lessons you can put it to work. Everything else on the site is there to look up when you need it.
</Callout>

## If something's off

<Q q="I've thought about it, and I can't come up with a goal.">

Then leave it blank for now and go straight to lesson 2. Once your bot exists, you can have it interview you, one question at a time, until the goal is clear. The message for that is in lesson 2, under "[If something's off](/en/start/2-first-bot/#interview)."

</Q>
<Q q="I have several jobs I want to hand off.">

Pick one first. A bot looks after one job, so everything it remembers is about that job, and you only explain it once. Give each of the other jobs its own bot later.

</Q>
<Q q="I have Grok on my phone. Is that it?">

No. Grok is an app for chatting. Grok Bot is a different app that you install separately, and lesson 2 walks you through it.

</Q>
<Q q="I'm not sure my plan includes it.">

On The X Subscription, Premium, Super and Ultra do, and Plus doesn't. Older paid Cursor individual plans and self-serve Cursor Teams include it too, and on one of those older Cursor accounts you can link a SuperGrok or X Premium+ subscription. If you're still unsure, see the official [pricing answer](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/faq#how-much-does-grok-bot-cost).

</Q>
<Q q="I only have a phone, no computer.">

These three lessons use the desktop app on a computer. The phone app (iPhone or Android) goes alongside it. Sign in there with the same account, and you can keep chatting with the same bots and approve what they want to do.

</Q>
