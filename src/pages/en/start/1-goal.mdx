Get a sheet of paper, or open the notes app on your phone. This lesson is one task: write one sentence. In the next lesson, you hand that sentence to Grok Bot.

## Pick a job you do again and again

Think of something you do every few days, on a computer or a phone. If nothing comes to mind, pick one from the author poteto's list:

- Save toward a new car, a house, or your kids' college fund
- Book new customers for your small business
- Give your small business's customers an experience good enough that they leave good reviews
- Hire a new employee for your small business
- Collect feedback on your product so you can improve it
- Prepare slides for a weekly meeting
- Build new features for your product

Write the job you picked on the paper. The rest of this track uses "slides for the weekly meeting" as the example. You write your own.

<Callout type="ok">
The paper has one job on it, like "slides for the weekly meeting."
</Callout>

## Write the result you want, not the steps

Under the job, write one sentence in this shape:

<mark><strong>The result I want: … · It's for: …</strong></mark>

For the meeting slides, that could be:

> The result I want: before Monday's meeting, everyone has slides that show this week's progress. It's for: spending the meeting on decisions instead of reading numbers aloud.

Why this shape: the author splits any job in two. The goal is what you want at the end. The process is how you get there.

- **Baking an apple pie.** The pie is the goal. Buying ingredients, putting the pie together, baking it and letting it cool are the process.
- **Changing a flat tire.** A car with a good tire is the goal. Jacking the car up, taking the old tire off and putting the new one on are the process.

Automation means a machine does the process, so you don't have to do it yourself every time. In Grok Bot, a <Term k="bot" /> has its own computer. It can move a mouse, click, use apps and browse the web. So it can do most of what you do on a computer. You describe the goal in plain words, and it works out the process with you.

<Callout type="ok">
Under the job there's a sentence in two halves: "The result I want" and "It's for."
</Callout>

## Check that you didn't write a step

The most common mistake is to treat one step of the process as the goal. "Fill in this form" and "send an email" are steps.

Look at what follows "The result I want" in your sentence. If it's an action like fill in, send, copy or click, ask what that action is for. Write the answer instead.

- **A step:** copy each team's update into the slides.
- **A goal:** before Monday's meeting, everyone has slides that show this week's progress.

For "It's for," think about what the job does for the business. Does it bring in money, or save money? If you're not sure, ask your manager or a colleague.

Your current way of doing it can change. The bot may find an easier way, as long as the goal is right.

<Callout type="ok">
"The result I want" is followed by a result, with no fill in, send, copy or click in it.
</Callout>

## If something's off

<Q q="I can't come up with a goal.">

Leave it blank and go to lesson 2. Once your bot exists, one short message gets it to interview you until the goal is clear. It's in lesson 2, under "[If something's off](/en/start/2-first-bot/#interview)."

</Q>
<Q q="I have several jobs I want to hand off.">

Pick one for now. A bot with one job keeps everything it learns about that job, so you only explain it once. Give each other job its own bot later.

</Q>
<Q q="I have Grok on my phone. Is that it?">

No. Grok is a chat app. Grok Bot is a different app that you install separately. Lesson 2 walks you through it.

</Q>
<Q q="I'm not sure my plan includes it.">

On The X Subscription, Premium, Super and Ultra do, and Plus doesn't. Older paid Cursor individual plans and Cursor Teams include it too. A personal SuperGrok or X Premium+ subscription can be linked to your account. If you're still unsure, see the official [pricing answer](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/faq#how-much-does-grok-bot-cost).

</Q>
<Q q="I only have a phone, no computer.">

These lessons use the desktop app on a computer. The phone app (iPhone or Android) goes with it. Sign in there with the same account. Then you can chat with the same bots and approve what they want to do.

</Q>
