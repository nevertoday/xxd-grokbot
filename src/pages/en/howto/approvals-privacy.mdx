This page covers the settings. For how approvals, apps and the computer fit together, see [Full guide step 4](/en/guide/4-apps/). The official page is [Approvals, security, and privacy](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/approvals-security-and-privacy).

## Say in the request where it stops

When you hand over a job, say how far it may go and where it waits for you:

<Copy id="ap-boundary" code="Go through next week's bookings, list the customers whose times need to change, and draft a text to each one. Don't send the texts or change the booking system. For each change, show the current time, the proposed time and why, and wait for my OK." />

It hands back a draft for you to decide on. These kinds of actions always deserve a stated boundary:

- **Sending messages or invitations**
- **Publishing content**
- **Purchases and transfers**
- **Deleting or overwriting data**
- **Changing permissions or production systems**
- **Accepting legal terms**

For a boundary that should last, ask it to remember:

<Copy id="ap-remember" code="From now on, ask me before you send a message to anyone. Remember this." />

Memory is only the first layer. For boundaries that matter, also add a rule as shown below.

## Read the card before you choose

When an action needs you, an <Term k="审批" text="approval" /> card shows what the bot wants to do and with what details. Check the target, scope and values, then choose:

| Button | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Allow once** | It runs now; no rule saved |
| **Always allow** | It runs now; similar ones run on their own |
| **Deny** | It doesn't run; no more asking until you write |

Some cards carry a warning, such as **This can't be undone**.

<Callout type="warn" title="If you can't tell, don't approve yet">
If you can't say what the card targets or does, ask the bot to explain it in plain words. Or ask for a draft first.
</Callout>

In a chat you started, the card waits for you. Cards from background work, like a routine or a message from another bot, expire after about 30 minutes. Then the action doesn't run. For bots that run routines, turn on **Notifications** so you don't miss them.

## Put Ask first on anything you can't undo

<mark>Anything you can't undo, like sending, paying or deleting, gets an Ask first rule.</mark> An approval only decides whether one action runs. It can't undo work that's done.

1. Open **Settings → General** and find **Custom Rules** under **Bot**. On the phone, it's in the **Bot** group of **Settings**.
2. Under **When Grok Bot wants to:**, describe one action in plain words.
3. Under **It should:**, choose **Ask first**, then **Add Rule**.

One rule per action, as narrow as you can make it:

- Ask me before emailing anyone outside the company.
- Ask me before changing a live dashboard.

The docs advise against broad rules like "allow everything in the browser," since websites and tools change. When both kinds of rule match, **Ask first** wins.

## Control access to your own computer

Bots normally work on the <Term k="云端电脑" text="cloud computer" />. The computer in front of you needs its own permission. Open **Settings → Computer → Your computer**, find **Execution on this computer**, and pick one:

- **Ask every time**: the default.
- **Always allow**: no more asking.
- **Never allow**: the docs suggest this unless a bot has a specific reason to touch your local files.

These settings only cover your own computer. Bots can still use the cloud computer.

## Remember: all your bots share one computer

Sites one bot signed in to, files it left, and command-line credentials are open to all your other bots. So:

- **Bots can't be walled off from each other.** Separate bots are not a security measure.
- **Sign out when you're done.** When a site shouldn't be used anymore, sign out of it.
- **Delete sensitive files after the job.** Clear out anything sensitive you put there for a task.
- **Clean up before you share.** When you share a bot template, remove keys, customer data and internal URLs.

A <Term k="team bot" text="Team Bot" /> is different again: the apps, secrets and files you add work in every teammate's chat.

## Your data and privacy

Grok Bot uses your Cursor account and that account's data settings.

- **It needs cloud storage.** Legacy Privacy Mode isn't supported.
- **Training opt-out** follows your Cursor account's privacy settings.
- **For contract details**, see the [Cursor Privacy Policy](https://cursor.com/privacy) and [security information](https://cursor.com/security).

## When you're done, take access back

When a project or a login should no longer be available to your bots, work through this list:

1. Pause or delete the related <Term k="routine" text="routines" />.
2. Sign out of those websites on the shared computer.
3. Remove the connected apps, then revoke their access in each service.
4. Delete sensitive files from `/workspace`.
5. Hide or delete bots you no longer use.

<Callout type="warn" title="Deleting a bot doesn't clean the computer">
Deleting a bot removes its profile, chats, memory and routines. Files and sign-ins on the shared computer stay, so clean them up with the steps above.
</Callout>

## Stuck?

<Q q="The same action asks for approval every time.">
Look in **Custom Rules** for a matching **Ask first** rule, including rules your team admin requires. **Ask first** wins over **Allow automatically**. For an action you trust, choose **Always allow** on the next card.
</Q>
<Q q="The card says Expired, and nothing happened.">
A card from background work expires if nobody answers, and the action doesn't run. Ask the bot to try again. If the card offers **Always allow next time**, choosing it saves a rule.
</Q>
<Q q="I can't turn off Auto Review.">
If the switch shows **Required by your admin**, your team admin requires it. Team rules always apply to you, and your own rules can only make things stricter.
</Q>
<Q q="It says local tools are off, but I allowed them.">
It says "Local tools are turned off," while your setting is **Always allow** or **Ask every time**. The docs say this is a known issue on Cursor's side. Your setting is correct, and restarting, reinstalling or changing it won't clear the message.
</Q>
