You've probably wondered about this. If you ask it to sort your inbox, will it go ahead and send the replies too? If you ask it to find flights, will it just book one? When you hand work to a bot, what you really need to control are exactly these things that can't be taken back once they're done: an email that's already gone to your landlord, money already paid for a flight, a spreadsheet already saved over with a new version. An approval only decides whether one action runs. It can't undo work that's done. <mark>So anything you can't undo, like sending, paying or deleting, gets an Ask first rule.</mark>

This page covers the settings. First you set a boundary in the request, then you read the approval card, then you add an Ask first rule, and last come your own computer, the shared computer, privacy, and taking access back. For how approvals, apps and the computer fit together, see [Full guide step 4](/en/guide/4-apps/). The official page is [Approvals, security, and privacy](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/approvals-security-and-privacy).

## Say in the request where it stops

The simplest boundary goes right into the request: how far it may go, and where it waits for you. For example:

<Copy id="ap-boundary" code="I want to take my parents away for three days next month. Find me two or three options for flights plus a hotel. Don't book anything. For each one, tell me the total, what time we'd leave, and whether it can be changed or refunded, and I'll tell you which one I pick." />

It hands back a few options. Once you've picked one, you decide how far it goes next. These kinds of actions always deserve a stated boundary:

- Sending messages or invitations
- Publishing content
- Purchases and transfers
- Deleting or overwriting data
- Changing permissions or production systems
- Accepting legal terms

For a boundary that should last, ask it to remember:

<Copy id="ap-remember" code="From now on, check with me before you send a message to anyone. Remember that." />

Memory is only the first layer, though. For boundaries that matter, also add a rule as shown below.

## Read the card before you choose

When an action needs you, an <Term k="审批" text="approval" /> card shows what the bot wants to do and with what details. Check the target, scope and values, meaning who it goes to, how many, and how much money, then choose:

| Button | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Allow once** | It runs now; no rule saved |
| **Always allow** | It runs now; similar ones run on their own |
| **Deny** | It doesn't run; no more asking until you write |

Some cards also carry a warning, such as **This can't be undone**.

<Callout type="warn" title="If you can't tell, don't approve yet">
If you can't say what the card targets or does, ask the bot to explain it in plain words, or ask for a draft first.
</Callout>

Whether a card waits for you depends on where the work came from. In a chat you started, the card waits. Cards from background work, like a routine or a message from another bot, expire after about 30 minutes, and then the action doesn't run. So for bots that run routines, turn on **Notifications** so you don't miss them.

## Put Ask first on anything you can't undo

In the settings, that opening sentence becomes an **Ask first** rule. One thing to know first: Custom Rules only work while **Auto-review** is on. It's on by default, and the switch is under **Bot** in **Settings → General**. If you turn it off, the bot runs actions without checking them first, and your rules are hidden and unused but kept. Turn it back on to use them again. See the official [Configure Auto Review](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/approvals-security-and-privacy#configure-auto-review).

1. Open **Settings → General** and find **Custom Rules** under **Bot**. On the phone, it's in the **Bot** group of **Settings**.
2. Under **When Grok Bot wants to:**, describe one action in plain words.
3. Under **It should:**, choose **Ask first**, then **Add Rule**.

One rule per action, as narrow as you can make it. For example:

- Ask me before emailing my kid's teacher.
- Ask me before sending a client an invoice.
- Ask me before deleting files in Google Drive.

The docs advise against broad rules like "allow everything in the browser," because websites and tools change. When an action matches both kinds of rule, **Ask first** wins.

## Control access to your own computer

Bots normally work on the <Term k="云端电脑" text="cloud computer" />, and the computer in front of you needs its own permission. Open **Settings → Computer → Your computer**, find **Execution on this computer**, and pick one of three:

- **Ask every time**: the default.
- **Always allow**: no more asking.
- **Never allow**: the docs suggest this unless a bot has a specific reason to touch your local files.

These settings only cover your own computer. Bots can still use the cloud computer.

## Remember: all your bots share one computer

There's one more thing to keep in mind. All your bots share the same cloud computer, so sites one bot signed in to, files it left, and command-line credentials are open to all your other bots. If your homework bot signed in to the school's course site, your job-hunting bot opens the browser and finds that site still signed in. So:

- Bots can't be walled off from each other, and separate bots are not a security measure.
- When a site shouldn't be used anymore, sign out of it.
- Delete sensitive files you put there for a task once the job is done.
- Before you share a bot template, clean it up by removing keys, customer data and internal URLs.

A <Term k="team bot" text="Team Bot" /> is different again: the apps, secrets and files you add work in every teammate's chat.

## Your data and privacy

Grok Bot uses your Cursor account and follows that account's data settings. It requires data storage, and Legacy Privacy Mode isn't supported. Training opt-out follows your Cursor account's privacy settings. For contract details, see the [Cursor Privacy Policy](https://cursor.com/privacy) and [security information](https://cursor.com/security).

## When you're done, take access back

You found a place to rent, so it can stop checking rental sites. You changed jobs, so it shouldn't touch your old work account. When a project or a login should no longer be available to your bots, work through this list:

1. Pause or delete the related <Term k="routine" text="routines" />.
2. Sign out of those websites on the shared computer.
3. Remove the connected apps, then revoke their access in each service.
4. Delete sensitive files from `/workspace`.
5. Hide or delete bots you no longer use.

<Callout type="warn" title="Deleting a bot doesn't clean the computer">
Deleting a bot erases its profile, chats, memory, routines and attachments, and it can't be undone. Files and sign-ins on the shared computer aren't kept per bot and can stay there, so clean them up with the steps above.
</Callout>

## Stuck?

<Q q="The same action asks for approval every time.">
First look in **Custom Rules** for a matching **Ask first** rule, including rules your team admin requires. **Ask first** wins over **Allow automatically**. If it's an action you trust, choose **Always allow** on the next card.
</Q>
<Q q="The card says Expired, and nothing happened.">
A card from background work expires if nobody answers, and the action doesn't run. Ask the bot to try again. If the card offers **Always allow next time**, choosing it saves a rule.
</Q>
<Q q="I can't turn off Auto Review.">
If the switch shows **Required by your admin**, your team admin requires it. Team rules always apply to you, and your own rules can only make things stricter.
</Q>
<Q q="It says local tools are off, but I allowed them.">
It says "Local tools are turned off," while your setting is **Always allow** or **Ask every time**. The docs say this is a known issue on Cursor's side. Your setting is correct, and restarting, reinstalling or changing it won't clear the message.
</Q>
