## Say what result you want

Answering every review is one step in the process. The result you want is customers happy enough to leave good reviews, and a clear cause for every bad one.

This is one of the goals the author, poteto, lists in the [article](https://x.com/poteto/status/2123159537016533493).

Make a new <Term k="bot" />, call it Potato, and send it the goal:

<Copy id="rf-goal" title="First message: the goal" code="Hi Potato. I want you to own this goal: customers are happy enough to leave us good reviews, and for every bad review we find out what went wrong. This is your job from now on, so remember it. Based on the goal, choose a name and a picture for yourself." />

## Tell it how you do it now

Tell it how you do this today. Change every line to fit your shop:

<Copy id="rf-process" title="Second message: your process" code={`Potato, this is how I usually do this myself:
- Steps: every day I read new reviews. I thank people for good ones. For bad ones, I find out what happened before I reply. Each week I group the complaints and see which kind comes up most.
- What I watch for: bad reviews, complaints about waiting, and reviews that name a staff member.
- Apps I use: Gmail. For review sites like Google and Yelp, use your own browser.
- How I check it's right: I read every reply before it goes out. Every suggested change links to the original review.
- Where the information comes from: review sites, customer emails, social media, and the shop's records for that day.
- Where feedback comes from: whether new reviews get better after we change something.

Think about how you could take this over from me completely. Before you begin, restate my goal and process in simple words, so I can give you feedback and confirm it. Don't start doing the work yet. Let's get the process planned first.`} />

If typing is slow, press `Cmd/Ctrl+D` and talk. Say things as they come to you. It restates what it heard first. Correct anything that's off, and go a few rounds.

When you're happy, tell it to go ahead. If it needs an app, it shows a **Connect** card in the chat. Open it and sign in.

## Teach it how you decide

You make some calls without thinking, and your bot can't know them. Here are three common ones for this job:

<Copy id="rf-judge" title="Third message: how you decide" code={`Potato, here are a few rules I use to decide:
1. For a bad review, look up that customer's records for the day before you write a reply.
2. Replies start with an apology, then say what we'll change. Don't argue in public. Refunds, discounts, and other promises wait for me.
3. A complaint counts as a problem to fix once the same kind shows up a second time. Note one-offs and wait.

Help me find what I haven't told you about this process yet. Ask me how I make decisions, where I get my information, how I check the work, and when you should ask me for help. Ask one question at a time. Then walk me through a few examples so I can give you feedback. Don't automate anything yet.`} />

- **Rule 1.** The article's example. A customer says on Yelp they waited a long time before anyone helped them. <mark>If the bot can read that review and that day's records, it can see what might have gone wrong.</mark>
- **Rule 2.** Promises that cost money stay with you, so a reply never commits you to anything.
- **Rule 3.** One-off complaints don't change how you work. Repeated ones do.

Include an example or two that went wrong, like the reply that made a customer angrier. See how it would handle them, then tell it what to fix.

<Callout type="tip" title="Not enough data? Have it build a log">
The article's example is a car repair shop. Ask the bot to build a small app for your computer and phone. Use it to log when a customer arrives, when they talk to a service rep, when you take their keys, and when service starts and finishes. With those times you can work out how long people waited and where. Link records across apps with one shared ID, like a customer email or an order number.
</Callout>

## Make it run on its own

Once this has gone well a few times, let it start on its own. Save the process as a <Term k="技能" /> first, then set up a <Term k="routine" />:

<Copy id="rf-routine" title="Fourth message: let it start on its own" code={`Save the process we just used as a skill called "Reviews."
Then set up a routine: every day at 9:00 AM, use that skill to read new reviews and post the reply drafts in this chat.
Don't publish any reply online yourself.
If a site won't open, tell me which one. Don't skip it quietly.`} />

After the first few runs, check these:

- **Every new review is there.** Count them on the site yourself.
- **Bad reviews got a records check.** Each draft for a bad review notes what it found for that day.
- **Nothing was published.** There are no replies from it on the site.

A new routine waits for its next scheduled time. To try it now, open it and choose **Test**. A test does real work. See [Create a routine](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations#create-a-routine) in the official docs.

For a weekly summary, set up a second routine the same way, like three suggested changes every Monday morning.

The official docs suggest keeping publishing behind an approval. To see what it asks you about first, read [Approvals and privacy](/en/howto/approvals-privacy/).

## Stuck?

<Q q="It can't get into the review site's business account.">
Save the login in <Term k="Vault" /> first, and the bot fills it in without seeing the password. If it still can't get in, like when a QR code is needed, it hands you the computer. Choose **Take over**, finish that step, and choose **I'm done**. Then tell it to continue.
</Q>
<Q q="The suggested changes are too vague.">
Ask it to link each suggestion to the original review and that day's records. Then ask it to keep three things apart: facts from the records, its guesses, and what it couldn't check.
</Q>
<Q q="I want to know if people mention us elsewhere online.">
It can search and read public posts on X without connecting an X account. The official marketplace also has [Overheard](https://x.ai/bot/marketplace/bots/overheard). It watches Reddit, Hacker News, news sites, and X for mentions of you. When something is worth reading, it sends a short weekday digest. It never posts for you.
</Q>
