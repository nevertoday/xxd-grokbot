Maybe you want your bot to check this month's electricity bill on the power company's site, look for new homework on the school's course site, or see whether anyone answered the job applications you sent. When it has to sign in, fill in details or check out for you, the quickest move seems to be sending it your password. Don't. <mark>Never send passwords, one-time codes or API keys in chat. Put logins and cards in <Term k="Vault" />, where bots can use them without seeing the password.</mark> The official page is [Vault and secure sign-in](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/vault).

Below, you'll save the logins you use often, then see where a bot looks when it signs in and how you take over for the step it can't do, and last come API keys and payments.

## Save the logins you use often

Start with the sites it visits most: the power and water companies whose bills you check every month, the site your kid's school posts notices on, the job or rental sites you check every day. For work, it's the few systems it signs in to daily.

1. Open **Settings → Vault** and choose **New item**.
2. Pick **Login**, **Card** or **Contact info**.
3. Fill it in and save. A login needs the **Website**, **Username or email** and **Password**, then **Save login**. For a card, **Scan with camera** or **Upload a photo** fills it in for you.

Under **Hints**, write when to use an entry. Say you look after your parents' power bill too, so you've saved two logins for the power company's site. On theirs, write "Use this one for my parents' bill." When there's more than one to choose from, the bot follows the hint.

<Callout type="ok">
After saving, passwords stay hidden and cards show only their last four digits. Saved values are never shown to you again.
</Callout>

Everything you save is available to every bot on your account, and you can't limit it to one. Bots never see passwords, but they can see which logins you saved, and your contact info. In chats in the Grok Bot app, a bot can also see a card's number and security code, so it can type them at checkout.

## Bring logins over from another password manager

On desktop you can import them in one go:

1. Export a CSV file from 1Password, Chrome or Edge, Safari or Apple Passwords, Firefox, Bitwarden or LastPass.
2. In **Settings → Vault**, choose **New item**, then **Import from CSV…**.
3. Choose **Choose CSV file** and pick the export. It's read on your computer, never uploaded, and must be under 5 MB.
4. Review the list, then choose the import button. Items you already have are skipped, and rows missing a password, or with a site that isn't https, aren't imported.

<u>The export holds your passwords unencrypted, so delete it as soon as the import finishes.</u>

You can also connect 1Password directly: open **1Password** in **Connect apps** and choose **Connect 1Password**. After that, you approve each login in 1Password. This integration is still rolling out gradually and needs 1Password 8.12.42 or later.

## When it signs in, it looks in this order

1. A login saved in **Vault**.
2. A password manager you connected, such as 1Password.
3. If neither has it, it asks you with a **Secure Form**.

The form names the site it fills. What you type goes straight into the page, and the bot never sees it. To skip it next time, check **Securely save to Bot's vault**. Leave it unchecked, and the value is used in that chat only and expires on its own.

When a site emails a one-time code, the bot can read it from a connected Gmail or Outlook account, or from its own inbox.

## For the step it can't do, you take over

Some steps only you can do, and then it hands you the computer. That includes single sign-on, a passkey and a CAPTCHA it can't get past, as well as an approval on another device, a QR code and a bank card check:

1. Choose **Take over** on the request in the chat, or open the computer and choose **Take control**.
2. Do only the step that's blocked.
3. Choose **I'm done**, then tell it to continue.

The signed-in session stays on the computer for next time, and all your bots can use it.

<Callout type="warn" title="Fingerprint and face sign-in don't work on its computer">
Fingerprint, Face ID, Windows Hello and passkeys saved on your laptop or phone don't work on the bot's computer. For a work app with single sign-on, take over and use another method your company allows, such as a password with a code. On Mac and Windows, you can also use a hardware security key: check that **Use hardware security keys** is on under **Security Key** in **Settings → General**. It is on by default on Mac and Windows, isn't supported on Linux yet, and every use asks you to approve it first.
</Callout>

## API keys go in the secure secret card

An API key is a long password some services give to programs, and you'll run into one when you build a website or connect tools. For an API key or a similar secret, wait for the bot to send a **secure secret card**. The field is masked, and after you choose **Save securely**, the card shows **Saved**. The value never appears in the chat, and the bot never sees it.

Also keep passwords, codes and API keys out of commands and files on the computer. When you use **Teach a task**, don't type secrets during the demonstration.

## Payments: to approve each one, don't save the card

Take the electricity bill. You probably want to see the amount each month before it's paid. But a card saved in Vault fills in at checkout without asking you each time, and no setting requires your approval for each use of the card. (The official Vault page also says that when a bot buys through an order card, it types the card in after you tap the pay button.) So to approve every payment yourself:

- **Don't save the card.** When the bot asks for a card with a Secure Form, leave **Securely save to Bot's vault** unchecked. The card is used in that chat only, and the bot asks you to approve the exact total there.
- **Remove a card you already saved.** In **Settings → Vault**, select it and choose **Remove**.

<u>A form without that checkbox saves straight to Vault. If you don't want the card saved, don't submit it.</u>

**Settings → Vault** also lists **Payment accounts**, where you can connect wallets such as Link, X Money or Shop Pay.

## Stuck?

<Q q="Where can I see a password I saved?">
You can't. Saved values are write-only: passwords stay hidden and cards show the last four digits. To change one, edit it in **Settings → Vault**. To change a card number, remove the card and add it again.
</Q>
<Q q="A website keeps asking me to sign in.">
Take over the computer, sign in yourself, and finish any two-factor step or CAPTCHA. Wait for the signed-in page to load, then hand back and tell the bot to continue from there. Some sites expire sessions quickly, and that can't always be avoided.
</Q>
<Q q="The form says Could not fill into the page.">
The page may have moved before the form could fill it, but your values still weren't shown to the bot. If it asks again, submit a new form.
</Q>
<Q q="A Team Bot can't use my saved logins.">
That's by design, since your Vault isn't available to Team Bots. The owner or a Manager can give it logins of its own: choose the wrench, then **Manage**, and find **Bot's accounts** under **General**. API keys go under **Secrets**.
</Q>
<Q q="Did a computer update delete my secrets?">
Updates, **Recover** and **Reset** remove apps and packages installed on the computer, so a secret you typed into a file or a shell profile can disappear with them. Secrets saved with the secure card, under **Secrets**, or in Vault are not affected.
</Q>
