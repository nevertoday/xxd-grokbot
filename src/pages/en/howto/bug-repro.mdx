## Say what result you want

To reproduce a bug means to make it happen again. Replying to the user is one step in the process. The result you want is that every bug reaches a developer with steps they can follow to see it.

The official docs call this Bug reproduction. See [Use cases](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/use-cases#bug-reproduction).

Make a new <Term k="bot" />, call it Potato, and send it the goal:

<Copy id="br-goal" title="First message: the goal" code="Hi Potato. I want you to own this goal: every bug users report reaches a developer with steps they can follow to see it. This is your job from now on, so remember it. Based on the goal, choose a name and a picture for yourself." />

## Tell it how you do it now

Tell it how you do this today. Change every line to fit your team:

<Copy id="br-process" title="Second message: your process" code={`Potato, this is how I usually do this myself:
- Steps: I read the user's report and follow it in staging with a fresh test account until the bug happens again. I write down every step, take screenshots, and note the browser and version. If it won't happen, I write down everything I tried.
- What I watch for: never use real customer data. Never change any production setting.
- Apps I use: tickets in Linear and feedback in Slack. Open staging in your own browser.
- How I check it's right: someone else can follow the steps and see the same bug.
- Where the information comes from: the ticket, the feedback channel in Slack, and screenshots from the user.
- Where feedback comes from: developers tell you whether the steps worked.

Think about how you could take this over from me completely. Before you begin, restate my goal and process in simple words, so I can give you feedback and confirm it. Don't start doing the work yet. Let's get the process planned first.`} />

If typing is slow, press `Cmd/Ctrl+D` and talk. Say things as they come to you. It restates what it heard first. Correct anything that's off, and go a few rounds.

When you're happy, tell it to go ahead. If it needs an app, it shows a **Connect** card in the chat. Open it and sign in.

## Teach it how you decide

You make some calls without thinking, and your bot can't know them. Here are three common ones for this job:

<Copy id="br-judge" title="Third message: how you decide" code={`Potato, here are a few rules I use to decide:
1. Even if it won't happen, bring something back: the environment, the account, and every approach you tried.
2. If the user's description and their screenshot disagree, put both in the report. Don't pick one.
3. If a step involves paying, deleting data, or changing permissions, stop and ask me before you do it.

Help me find what I haven't told you about this process yet. Ask me how I make decisions, where I get my information, how I check the work, and when you should ask me for help. Ask one question at a time. Then walk me through a few examples so I can give you feedback. Don't automate anything yet.`} />

- **Rule 1.** <mark>A failed repro still helps, because developers don't repeat what was already tried.</mark>
- **Rule 2.** Both accounts stay in the report, and the developer decides.
- **Rule 3.** Staging can still touch real things, so those steps go to you.

Include an example or two that went wrong, like the bug that only showed up in one browser. See how it would handle them, then tell it what to fix.

## Make it run on its own

Once this has gone well a few times, let it start on its own. Save the process as a <Term k="技能" /> first, then set up a <Term k="routine" />.

This job starts on a Slack message. When someone writes "needs repro" in the channel, it starts.

<Copy id="br-routine" title="Fourth message: let it start on its own" code={`Save the process we just used as a skill called "Bug repro."
Then set up a routine: when a message in #bug-reports has a ticket link and says "needs repro," open the ticket, use that skill to reproduce it in staging, and post the steps, screenshots, and browser details in this chat.
Never post back to Slack without my approval.`} />

Keep the <Term k="触发条件" /> narrow: one channel and one phrase. Watching every message is noisy and spends a lot of usage. A Slack trigger needs Slack connected on the Cursor account that owns the bot. See [Slack triggers](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations#slack-triggers) in the official docs.

After the first few runs, check these:

- **The steps work.** Follow them yourself and see the bug.
- **The screenshots show it.** They capture the moment it goes wrong.
- **No real data, no Slack replies.** It used a test account, and Slack has nothing from it.

To try it now, post a message with a ticket link and "needs repro" in that channel.

To see what it asks you about first, read [Approvals and privacy](/en/howto/approvals-privacy/).

## Stuck?

<Q q="It's asking for the test account password.">
Save it in <Term k="Vault" />, or enter it in a Secure Form when the bot shows one. Either way, the bot never sees the password. Never paste a password into the chat.
</Q>
<Q q="Someone wrote needs repro, and nothing happened.">
Check three things:

- The message is newer than the routine.
- It's in the channel the routine watches, and it has the phrase.
- Slack is connected on the account that owns the bot.

Open the routine, and **When to run** spells out the channel and phrase it watches.
</Q>
<Q q="Staging won't load.">
Some sites drop connections from the bot's computer. Open **Settings → Computer**, and under **Your computer** turn on **Route traffic through this computer**. If staging is on your company's private network, this setting won't reach it. An admin has to install a networking client for the bot, and only the Enterprise plan offers that. See [Connect to private networks](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/private-networks) in the official docs.
</Q>
