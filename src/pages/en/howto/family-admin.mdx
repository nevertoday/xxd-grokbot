The kids' school sends a few notices a week, about parent meetings, money to hand in, and what to bring tomorrow, and the after-school classes send their own. Your parents have a follow-up appointment next month and a prescription to pick up every month, and you're the only one keeping track of the dates. Add when this month's electricity bill is due, and something always slips until it's too late. When you finish this page, your bot will post a list in the chat every evening at 8:00 PM of what's coming up from tomorrow through next week, sorted by date, each item linked to its email. Any replies it writes are drafts that wait for you to send.

<mark>Remembering goes to the bot. Your calendar, your replies, and your payments stay your call.</mark>

It takes four steps: give it the goal, explain how you do it now, teach it how you decide, then let it start on its own.

## The goal isn't answered email

Answering email is only one step in the process. What you actually want is that nothing for the kids' school, your parents' health, or the household bills slips. Make a new <Term k="bot" />, call it Potato, and tell it where you're at and what you want:

<Copy id="fa-goal" title="First message: the goal" code="Hi Potato. I'm the one who keeps track of everything at home. The two kids' school and after-school classes send a few notices a week, my parents have checkups and prescriptions every month, and then there are all the bills. Last month I forgot a parent meeting, and I paid the electricity bill late too. I'd like you to take this over: make sure nothing at home slips. That way I'm not waking up at midnight remembering something. This is your job from now on, so remember it. Then pick a name and a picture for yourself that fit the job." />

<Callout type="tip" title="Want a shortcut? Use a ready-made bot">
The official bot marketplace has [Tradbot](https://x.ai/bot/marketplace/bots/tradbot-2) for exactly this. It watches your personal email and calendar, drafts replies, and catches pickup clashes. Its listing says it never sends without you. The rest of this page teaches your own bot from scratch.
</Callout>

## Tell it how you keep track today

Now tell it how you do this today: which kinds of email you pick out, which calendar you check, and how you order the list. Here's an example. Swap in your own family's details:

<Copy id="fa-process" title="Second message: your process" code={`Potato, here's how I do it now. Every evening I go through my personal email and pick out anything from school, the after-school classes, the doctor's office, or a bill. Then I check the shared family calendar to see if pickups, appointments, and everything else fit together, and last I make a list sorted by date. What I'm scared of missing: anything that needs a signature or a form, anything that needs money, anything the kids have to bring, and the dates for my parents' checkups and prescriptions. I use Gmail and Google Calendar, and some notices only show up on the school website. It's right when every item on the list has a date and a link to the original email. When someone in the family tells me I missed something, I'll pass it on to you.

Think about how you could take this off my plate completely. Before you start, tell me back in plain words what you think the goal is and how you'd go about it, and I'll tell you if you've got it. Don't do anything yet. Let's get the plan right first.`} />

If you look after your parents and there are no kids at home, just leave out the school and after-school classes and keep the doctor, the pharmacy, and the bills.

If typing is slow, press `Cmd/Ctrl+D` and just talk, saying things as they come to you. It restates what it heard first. Correct whatever's off and go a few rounds until it's right. When you're happy, tell it to go ahead. If it needs an app, it shows a **Connect** card in the chat. Open it and sign in.

## What it can do, and what stays your call

Some of this the bot can actually do. It can edit your calendar, and it can answer invitations. But family plans are yours to make, so spell out the rules first:

<Copy id="fa-judge" title="Third message: how you decide" code={`Potato, here are a few rules I go by. Anything in the next three days goes at the top, with tomorrow's things marked on their own, and the rest goes by date. If a pickup or an appointment clashes with something, tell me first. Don't change the calendar for me, and don't answer anyone's invitation for me. Bills get a reminder, never a payment. I pay them myself.

Also, help me figure out what I haven't told you yet: how I make calls, where my info comes from, how I know it's done right, and when you should come to me. One question at a time. Then walk me through a few examples and I'll tell you what's off. Don't set anything to run on its own yet.`} />

The first rule brings what's due soon to the top, so you see tomorrow's things the night before and even a long list doesn't hide anything. The other two draw the line: it reminds, you decide. Keep in mind, though, that "bills get a reminder, never a payment" is only a rule in your prompt. To make sure it can't pay, don't save a card in <Term k="Vault" />, because a saved card fills in at checkout without asking you.

When it's done asking, try it on a real case or two that went wrong, like the permission slip that almost expired, or the afternoon a checkup landed on the same day as a parent meeting. See how it would handle them, then tell it what to fix.

## A list every evening at 8

Once this has gone well a few times, you can let it start on its own. First have it save the process as a <Term k="技能" />, written instructions it follows every time, then set up a <Term k="routine" /> that tells it when to start:

<Copy id="fa-routine" title="Fourth message: let it start on its own" code={`Save what we just worked out as a skill called "Family admin." Then set up a routine: every evening at 8:00 PM, use that skill to list what's coming up from tomorrow through next week, and post it in this chat. Write any replies as drafts for me to send. Don't pay for anything, and don't accept or decline any invitation for me.`} />

After the first few runs, first check that no ads slipped in, since promotional email shouldn't count as a bill. Then pick two items and compare their dates with the original email. Last, make sure nothing was paid or answered, and the replies are still drafts.

A new routine waits for its next scheduled time. If you'd rather not wait, open it and choose **Test**, but keep in mind that a test does real work. See [Create a routine](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations#create-a-routine) in the official docs. For what it stops to ask you about, see [Approvals and privacy](/en/howto/approvals-privacy/).

## Before a checkup, put the test results in one table

At a follow-up, the doctor asks what a number was last time, and you're scrolling through your camera roll and can't find the sheet. The bot can do this one too. Take photos of the last few test results, or drag the PDFs the clinic sent into the chat, and have it put them in one table you bring along.

<Copy id="fa-checkups" title="Put test results in a table" code={`These are my dad's last three test results, some photos and some PDFs. Put them in one table: one row per test, the three results side by side, and anything the sheet marks as out of range, mark the same way. Just organize them. Don't draw any conclusions for me. If a cell is hard to read, leave it blank and flag it for me. Don't change the original files, and send me the table when it's done.`} />

The table shows up as a card in the chat, which you can open to preview and save. When you get it, check two or three numbers against the original sheets before you take it along.

Hand it the remembering, keep the deciding, and the family stuff stops slipping.

## Stuck?

<Q q="It can't see other family members' calendars.">
The Google Calendar app reads only the calendars your Google account can see, so ask your family to share their calendars with your account first.
</Q>
<Q q="It treated a promotional email as a bill.">
Show it two real emails, one bill and one promotion. Explain how to tell them apart, then add "keep doing this from now on, remember it."
</Q>
<Q q="The school website wants a login.">
Save the login in <Term k="Vault" />, and the bot fills it in without seeing the password. If it isn't saved, the bot shows a Secure Form for you to fill in, and the values go straight into the page. Never paste a password into the chat.
</Q>
<Q q="It can't read a photo of a test result.">
It may not read files that are large, encrypted, damaged, or in an unusual format. Take a clearer photo, or export the file as a PDF or an image.
</Q>
