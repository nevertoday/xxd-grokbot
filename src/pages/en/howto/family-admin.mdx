The kids' school sends a few notices a week, about parent-teacher meetings, fees to pay, and what to bring tomorrow, and the after-school classes send their own. Your parents have a follow-up appointment next month and a prescription to pick up every month, and you're the only one keeping track of the dates. Add when this month's electricity bill is due, and something always slips until it's too late. When you finish this page, your bot will post a list in the chat every evening at 8:00 PM of what's coming up from tomorrow through next week, sorted by date, each item linked to its email. Any replies it writes are drafts that wait for you to send.

<mark>Remembering goes to the bot. Your calendar, your replies, and your payments stay your call.</mark>

## The goal isn't answered email

Answering email is just one piece of it. What you want is that nothing for the kids' school, your parents' health, or the household bills slips. Make a new <Term k="bot" />, call it Potato, and tell it what home looks like for you:

<Copy id="fa-goal" title="First message: the goal" code="I'm the one who keeps track of everything at home. The two kids' school and after-school classes send a few notices a week, my parents have checkups and prescriptions every month, and then there are all the bills. Last month I forgot a parent-teacher meeting, and I paid the electricity bill late too. From now on you keep track of all that for me, so nothing slips and I'm not waking up at midnight remembering something. This is a long-term job, so make a note of it." />

It's still called Potato at this point. For a name that fits the job, end with one more line. [Beginner lesson 2](/en/start/2-first-bot/) has one you can use.

<Callout type="tip" title="Want a shortcut? Use a ready-made bot">
The official bot marketplace has [Tradbot](https://x.ai/bot/marketplace/bots/tradbot-2) for exactly this. It watches your personal email and calendar, drafts replies, and catches pickup clashes. Its listing says it never sends without you. The rest of this page teaches your own bot from scratch.
</Callout>

## Tell it how you keep track today

Now tell it how you pull all this together each evening. Here's what one parent might say. Swap in your own family's details:

<Copy id="fa-process" title="Second message: your process" code={`Most nights, once the kids are asleep, I go through my personal email from the top and pull out anything from school, the after-school classes, the doctor's office, or a bill. Then I open the calendar the whole family shares to see if pickups, appointments, and everything else fit together, and I make a list sorted by date. What I'm scared of missing: anything that needs a signature or a form, anything that needs money, anything the kids have to bring, and the dates for my parents' checkups and prescriptions. Some notices only go up on the school website, and you have to log in to see them. Every item on my list has a date and the original email, which is how I double-check it. When someone in the family tells me I missed something, I'll pass it on to you.

Figure out how you'd take all of it off my hands. Stay out of my calendar for now, and just walk me through your plan.`} />

If you look after your parents and there are no kids at home, just leave out the school and after-school classes and keep the doctor, the pharmacy, and the bills.

With Gmail and Google Calendar, a **Connect** card shows up in the chat the first time it needs each one. Open it and sign in. Too tired to type at night? Press `Cmd/Ctrl+D` and talk. When what it describes isn't what you meant, correct it on the spot, and after a few rounds it'll have it.

## What it can do, and what stays your call

Some of this the bot can actually do. It can edit your calendar, and it can answer invitations. But family plans are yours to make, so spell out the rules first:

<Copy id="fa-judge" title="Third message: how you decide" code={`Here are the house rules. Anything due within three days goes at the top, with tomorrow's things marked on their own, and the rest goes by date. If a pickup or an appointment clashes with something, tell me first. Don't change the calendar for me, and don't answer anyone's invitation for me. Bills get a reminder, and I pay them myself.

I'm sure I haven't covered every kind of notice, like the school's photos from a field trip, or a clinic's ad for a health check. Whenever you're not sure something belongs on the list, check with me, a single notice at a time.`} />

The first rule brings what's due soon to the top, so you see tomorrow's things the night before and even a long list doesn't hide anything. The other two draw the line: it reminds, you decide. Keep in mind, though, that "bills get a reminder, and I pay them myself" is only a rule in your prompt. To make sure it can't pay, don't save a card in <Term k="Vault" />, because a saved card fills in at checkout without asking you.

When it's done asking, test it on things that really went wrong: the reply slip that almost missed its date, or the afternoon a checkup landed on the same day as a parent-teacher meeting. Hear how it would handle them, and correct it right there. You're still watching each run here, so don't set it to run on its own yet. For house rules you haven't thought of, send [the one on the prompts page](/en/howto/prompts/#p-gaps) and let it ask you.

## A list every evening at 8

Once it has sorted a few evenings without a slip, let it do this every night on its own. First have it save the process as a <Term k="技能" />, the instructions it works from, then set up a <Term k="routine" /> that says when to start:

<Copy id="fa-routine" title="Fourth message: let it start on its own" code={`Make all that a skill and call it "Family admin." Next, a routine: every evening at 8:00 PM, use it to list what's coming up from tomorrow through next week, and post it here. Write any replies as drafts for me to send. Don't pay a cent for anything, and don't accept or turn down anyone's invitation for me.`} />

After the first few runs, first check that no ads slipped in, since promotional email shouldn't count as a bill. Then pick two items and compare their dates with the original email. Last, make sure nothing was paid or answered, and the replies are still drafts.

A routine you just set up first runs at the next 8:00 PM. To see it work now, open it and choose **Test**. A test really goes through your email and makes the list, and any replies are still only drafts. See [Create a routine](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations#create-a-routine) in the official docs. For what it stops to ask you about, see [Approvals and privacy](/en/howto/approvals-privacy/).

## Before a checkup, put the test results in one table

At a follow-up, the doctor asks what a number was last time, and you're scrolling through your camera roll and can't find the sheet. The bot can do this one too. Take photos of the last few test results, or drag the PDFs the clinic sent into the chat, and have it put them in one table you bring along.

<Copy id="fa-checkups" title="Put test results in a table" code={`These are my dad's last three test results, some photos and some PDFs. Put them in one table: one row per test, the three results side by side, and anything the sheet marks as out of range, mark the same way. Just organize them. Don't draw any conclusions for me. If a cell is hard to read, leave it blank and flag it for me. Don't change the original files, and send me the table when it's done.`} />

The table shows up as a card in the chat, which you can open to preview and save. When you get it, check two or three numbers against the original sheets before you take it along.

Hand it the remembering, keep the deciding, and the family stuff stops slipping.

## Stuck?

<Q q="It can't see other family members' calendars.">
The Google Calendar app reads only the calendars your Google account can see, so ask your family to share their calendars with your account first.
</Q>
<Q q="It treated a promotional email as a bill.">
Show it two real emails, one bill and one promotion. Explain how to tell them apart, then add "keep doing this from now on, remember it."
</Q>
<Q q="The school website wants a login.">
Save the login in <Term k="Vault" />, and the bot fills it in without seeing the password. If it isn't saved, the bot shows a Secure Form for you to fill in, and the values go straight into the page. Never paste a password into the chat.
</Q>
<Q q="It can't read a photo of a test result.">
It may not read files that are large, encrypted, damaged, or in an unusual format. Take a clearer photo, or export the file as a PDF or an image.
</Q>
