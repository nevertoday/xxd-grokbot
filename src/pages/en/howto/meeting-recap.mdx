By the end of this page you'll have a bot that wraps up your meetings. When the day's calls are over, it drafts a list of who has to finish what by when, and nothing goes to the team until you've read it and said yes.

You've probably sat through this one. The meeting is lively, everyone nods, and an hour later nobody can say who took which job, while the recap waits for someone to write it. But the recap was never really what you wanted. It's one step in the process. What you want is for everyone to walk out knowing what they have to finish and by when. <mark>So the goal you hand your bot is that everything the meeting decides has an owner and a date.</mark>

## Hand it the result, not the recap

Make a new <Term k="bot" /> and give it any name. If nothing comes to mind, call it Potato and let it rename itself later. Then send it the goal:

<Copy id="mr-goal" title="First message: the goal" code="Hi Potato. I want everyone to leave every meeting knowing what they have to finish and by when, so that nothing we agree on gets dropped just because nobody remembered it. This is your job from now on, so remember it. Based on the goal, choose a name and a picture for yourself." />

<Callout type="warn" title="Joining meetings depends on your plan">
A bot can join meetings on Team, Enterprise, Ultra, and Super plans. See [Meetings](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/meetings) in the official docs. If your plan doesn't have it, that's fine. Paste or upload your meeting notes instead, and the rest of this page still works.
</Callout>

## Tell it how you wrap up a meeting

With the goal set, tell it how you do this yourself today. The easiest way is to press `Cmd/Ctrl+D` and talk, saying things as they come to you without tidying them first. Here's an example. Swap in your own apps and habits:

<Copy id="mr-process" title="Second message: your process" code={`Potato, here's how I usually do this myself. I take notes during the meeting, and afterward I write down what we decided, who's doing what, and by when, and send it to everyone who came. Before the next meeting I check in with each person on progress. Two things matter most to me: every action item has an owner and a date, and the recap only says what was actually said. I use Google Calendar to see the meetings and who's attending, Slack to send things to the team, and Notion to keep the recaps. The information comes from the meeting itself (I'll give you the link), the attendees on the calendar invite, and my own notes. How do I know it's right? Every action item can be traced back to the meeting record. When something's wrong, my teammates tell me.

Think about how you could take this over from me completely. Before you begin, restate my goal and process in simple words, so I can give you feedback and confirm it. Don't start doing the work yet. Let's get the process planned first.`} />

It restates what it heard first. Getting it wrong the first time is normal, so correct whatever's off and go a few rounds until you're happy, then tell it to go ahead. If it needs an app, it shows a **Connect** card in the chat. Open it and sign in.

## Where it shouldn't decide for you

Some calls you make without thinking, like who gets the item nobody claimed in the meeting. You just know. Your bot doesn't, so it would have to guess, and one wrong owner is enough to make people stop trusting the recap. So tell it your rules too:

<Copy id="mr-judge" title="Third message: how you decide" code={`Potato, here are a few rules I use to decide. If nobody clearly owns an action item, mark it "owner TBD" and ask me. Don't assign it to anyone yourself. Write only what was said in the meeting, and never make up a quote. For meetings with just our own people, draft the recap as a post for the team channel. If a customer was there, send it only to me.

Then help me find what I haven't told you about this process yet. Ask me how I make decisions, where I get my information, how I check the work, and when you should come to me. Ask one question at a time. Then walk me through a few examples so I can give you feedback. Don't automate anything yet.`} />

When you pick examples, include one or two that went wrong, like the action item with the wrong owner. See how it would handle them, then tell it what to fix.

## Let it wrap up every day on its own

Once this has gone well a few times, it can start on its own. First save the process as a <Term k="技能" />, then set up a <Term k="routine" />, which is a job it starts by itself on a schedule.

A recurring meeting doesn't actually need a routine. Paste the meeting link and say "join every meeting in this series and take notes," and it schedules that itself. The routine is for the daily wrap-up:

<Copy id="mr-routine" title="Fourth message: let it start on its own" code={`Save the process we just worked out as a skill called "Meeting recap." Then set up a routine: every weekday at 6:00 PM, use that skill to list today's action items from all my meetings, and draft one Slack post for #team. Don't send it until I approve. If a meeting has no record, name it. Don't fill it in from memory.`} />

When it needs your approval, a card shows up in the chat. Approve it. Once the meeting starts, the host lets it in, and its name ends in "transcribing." When the call ends, it posts a summary and action items in its chat.

After the first few runs, look over what it made. Every item should have an owner and a date, and any without an owner should say "owner TBD." Pick one item and ask it to show you that part of the meeting record, to make sure no quote was made up. Then glance at Slack to check that the draft isn't there yet.

A new routine waits for its next scheduled time. To try it now, open it and choose **Test**, but know that a test does real work. See [Create a routine](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations#create-a-routine) in the official docs.

It helps before meetings too. Ask for a one-page brief on who's attending, where you left off last time, and what's still open. See [Meeting prep](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/use-cases#meeting-prep) in the official docs. To see what it stops to ask you about first, read [Approvals and privacy](/en/howto/approvals-privacy/).

## Stuck?

<Q q="The bot can't get into the meeting.">
First check the chat for a card waiting on your approval, then check that the host let it in. It shows up as "Bot name (your first name's Bot) - transcribing."
</Q>
<Q q="I don't want it to talk in the meeting.">
Open the bot's details, find the meeting under **Upcoming**, open its menu, and choose **Disable Interactivity**. It still listens and takes notes, it just doesn't answer in the meeting, and the row shows **Muted**.
</Q>
<Q q="My plan doesn't include meetings.">
Then paste your meeting notes into the chat, or upload them as a file. If you've connected Granola, it can read your Granola notes too. The official marketplace also has [Meeting Recap Deck](https://x.ai/bot/marketplace/bots/echo), which turns notes into a recap deck in your slide template and never invents a quote.
</Q>
