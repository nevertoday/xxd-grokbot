Once your shop posts a job, applications trickle into your inbox. Some people can work weekends, some can't start until next month, and setting up interviews means moving times back and forth. When you finish this page, your bot will screen the day's new applications against your must-haves every weekday at 5:00 PM, add them to an applicant list, and pick interview times from the free slots on your calendar. Invitations and rejections are drafts that wait for you to send.

One rule matters more than the rest. <mark>Name the must-haves first, so one nice extra never lets someone through without them.</mark>

Hiring is one of the goals the author, poteto, lists in the [article](https://x.com/poteto/status/2123159537016533493). The official docs have a similar use case called a Talent scout. See [Use cases](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/use-cases#talent-scout). It takes four steps: give it the goal, explain how you do it now, teach it how you decide, then let it start on its own.

## The goal isn't a job post, it's a hire

Posting the job is only one step in the process. What you actually want is a hire who stays, with your own time spent only on interviews. Make a new <Term k="bot" />, call it Potato, and give it that goal:

<Copy id="hr-goal" title="First message: the goal" code="Hi Potato. There's a goal I'd like you to own. I want to hire someone for the shop who will stay for the long run, so that I only have to spend my own time on interviews. This is your job from now on, so please remember it. Then, based on the goal, choose a name and a picture for yourself." />

## Tell it how you screen and schedule

Now tell it how you do this today: how you screen, how you set up interviews, and how you keep track. Here's an example. Swap in your own shop's details:

<Copy id="hr-process" title="Second message: your process" code={`Potato, this is how I usually do this myself. I collect applications and screen them against the must-haves. Good fits get an interview, and the rest get a polite no. After each interview I tell you the outcome, and you reply to the person. The must-haves are weekend shifts and a start date within a month, and everyone who applies hears back. I use Gmail, Google Calendar, and a Google Sheets list of applicants. Applications come into my email, and some people leave messages on the job site. It's right when everyone is on the list with a status, and interviews are on my calendar with the applicant confirmed. After each interview I'll give you my notes on the person.

Think about how you could take this over from me completely. Before you begin, restate my goal and process in simple words, so I can give you feedback and confirm it. Don't start doing the work yet. Let's get the process planned first.`} />

If typing is slow, press `Cmd/Ctrl+D` and just talk, saying things as they come to you. It restates what it heard first. Correct whatever's off and go a few rounds until it's right. When you're happy, tell it to go ahead. If it needs an app, it shows a **Connect** card in the chat. Open it and sign in.

## Keep must-haves and nice-to-haves apart

When you read an application, you know exactly which things are deal-breakers and which are just a bonus. The bot can't tell them apart, so tell it:

<Copy id="hr-judge" title="Third message: how you decide" code={`Potato, here are a few rules I use to decide. Keep must-haves and nice-to-haves apart. Anyone missing a must-have gets a draft rejection, and nice-to-haves only set the order. Book interviews only in free slots on my calendar, with 15 minutes between them. If something in an application is unclear, like a gap in work history, list it and ask me. Don't guess.

Help me find what I haven't told you about this process yet. Ask me how I make decisions, where I get my information, how I check the work, and when you should ask me for help. Ask one question at a time. Then walk me through a few examples so I can give you feedback. Don't automate anything yet.`} />

The first rule is the line from the top of this page. However good the extras look, a missing must-have still means no. The second has it book against your calendar, so interviews don't clash with your other plans. The third sends unclear cases to you, so it never makes calls about people for you.

When it's done asking, try it on a real case or two that went wrong, like the applicant who said on interview day they couldn't do weekends. See how it would handle them, then tell it what to fix.

## Every weekday at 5 PM, it screens the new ones

Once this has gone well a few times, you can let it start on its own. First have it save the process as a <Term k="技能" />, written instructions it follows every time, then set up a <Term k="routine" /> that tells it when to start:

<Copy id="hr-routine" title="Fourth message: let it start on its own" code={`Save the process we just used as a skill called "Hiring." Then set up a routine: every weekday at 5:00 PM, use that skill on today's new applications and update the applicant list. Write interview invitations and rejections as drafts for me to send. Don't contact anyone for me, and don't send calendar invitations.`} />

After the first few runs, first check that the list is complete, with every application from today in the sheet. Then compare the interview times it picked with your calendar to make sure they're really free. Last, make sure nothing was sent, and invitations and rejections are still drafts.

A new routine waits for its next scheduled time. If you'd rather not wait, open it and choose **Test**, but keep in mind that a test does real work. See [Create a routine](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations#create-a-routine) in the official docs. For what it stops to ask you about, see [Approvals and privacy](/en/howto/approvals-privacy/).

Get the must-haves straight, and your time goes only to the people worth meeting.

## Stuck?

<Q q="It can't open a resume attachment.">
It may not read encrypted, password-protected, or unusual files. Ask the applicant to send a PDF instead.
</Q>
<Q q="It booked an interview over something else.">
The Google Calendar app sees only the calendars your account can see. If your plans live in several calendars, tell it which ones to check.
</Q>
<Q q="I want applicants to email the bot directly.">
Give the bot its own email address, then use a plus alias like `yourname+jobs@mail.grokbot.com`. A routine that watches the alias runs only for mail sent to that alias. Anyone can write to an alias, though, so the bot still screens everyone against the must-haves.
</Q>
