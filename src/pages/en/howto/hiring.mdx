Weekends are the busiest time at your pet grooming shop, and you want to hire some part-time help. Once the job is posted, applications trickle into your inbox. Some people can only do weekdays, some can't start until next month, and some don't say whether they're OK around dogs at all. Setting up interviews means moving times back and forth, and by the end of the week you're tired from the email alone. It's the same if you're hiring a weekend clerk for a phone repair counter or a part-time tutor. When you finish this page, your bot will screen the day's new applications against your must-haves every weekday at 5:00 PM, add them to an applicant list, and pick interview times from the free slots on your calendar. Invitations and rejections are drafts that wait for you to send.

One rule matters more than the rest. <mark>Name the must-haves first, so one nice extra never lets someone through without them.</mark>

Hiring is one of the goals the author, poteto, lists in the [article](https://x.com/poteto/status/2123159537016533493). The official docs have a similar use case called a Talent scout. See [Use cases](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/use-cases#talent-scout).

## The goal isn't a job post, it's a hire

Posting the job is only the first step. What you want is a hire who stays, with your own time spent only on interviews. Make a new <Term k="bot" />, call it Potato, and tell it why the shop needs someone:

<Copy id="hr-goal" title="First message: the goal" code="Potato, I run a pet grooming shop, we're swamped on weekends, and I need part-time help. Applications keep coming in, and I have to read every one, answer every one, and then keep moving interview times around. It's wearing me out. Hiring is yours to run now: find me someone who'll stick around, and I'll just show up for the interviews. Remember that this whole thing is on you from today." />

If you want it to name itself after the job, tack a line onto the end. [Beginner lesson 2](/en/start/2-first-bot/) shows how.

## Tell it how you screen and schedule

Now tell it how you screen, how you set up interviews, and how you keep track. Here's what the grooming shop owner might say. Swap in your own must-haves:

<Copy id="hr-process" title="Second message: your process" code={`When an application comes in, I check three must-haves first: free both weekend days, can start within a month, and fine around big dogs. If they pass, I set up an interview. If not, they get a polite no. Either way, everyone hears back. After each interview I'll tell you how it went and what I thought of the person, and you write back to them. Applications land in my email, and some people leave messages on the job site. I keep a sheet of who applied and how far they've got. Interviews go on my calendar, and one only counts as set when the applicant has confirmed.

Picture running this start to finish yourself. Don't write to anyone yet. Tell me how you'd screen and how you'd book interviews, and I'll listen for what's off.`} />

At a phone repair counter, the must-haves might be weekend availability and knowing how to put on a screen protector. For a tutoring center, it might be which subjects someone can teach and how late they're free in the evening. Use your own few.

The apps here are Gmail, Google Calendar, and a Google Sheets applicant list. If one isn't connected yet, it shows a **Connect** card in the chat. Open it and sign in. It reads the job site in the browser on its own computer. If its plan is off somewhere, say so, and a few rounds will get it right.

## Keep must-haves and nice-to-haves apart

When you read an application, you know exactly which things are deal-breakers and which are just a bonus. The bot can't tell them apart, so tell it:

<Copy id="hr-judge" title="Third message: how you decide" code={`When you read applications, go by these. Keep must-haves and nice-to-haves apart. Anyone missing a must-have gets a draft rejection. Nice-to-haves, like having owned a dog or done grooming before, only decide the order. Only book interviews in free slots on my calendar, with 15 minutes between them. If something in an application doesn't make sense, like a stretch of time with nothing listed, put it on a list and ask me. Don't guess.

Whenever an application leaves you unsure, like someone who can only do half days, or a student who's only free during school breaks, save it up and ask me about them one by one.`} />

The first rule is the line from the top of this page. However good the extras look, a missing must-have still means no. The second has it book against your calendar, so interviews don't clash with your other plans. The third sends unclear cases to you, so it never makes calls about people for you.

Then test it on the applicant who said on interview day that they couldn't do Sundays. In its place, would it have spotted the signs, and what would it do? Correct it where it's wrong. While the rules are still settling, don't set it to run on its own. To have it ask about whatever you left out, use [this prompt on the prompts page](/en/howto/prompts/#p-gaps).

## Every weekday at 5 PM, it screens the new ones

Once you're happy with a few batches, let it do this on a schedule. First save the process as a <Term k="技能" />, the instructions it follows from now on, then set up a <Term k="routine" /> that says when it starts:

<Copy id="hr-routine" title="Fourth message: let it start on its own" code={`Save this process as a skill called "Hiring." Then add a routine: every weekday at 5:00 PM, use it on today's new applications and bring the applicant list up to date. Interview invitations and rejections are drafts until I send them. Don't get in touch with anyone for me, and no calendar invitations either.`} />

After the first few runs, first check that the list is complete, with every application from today in the sheet. Then compare the interview times it picked with your calendar to make sure they're really free. Last, make sure nothing was sent, and invitations and rejections are still drafts.

Set it up after 5:00 PM, and its first run is the next weekday. Want to see it now? Open it and choose **Test**. A test really screens today's applications, and the invitations and rejections are still only drafts. See [Create a routine](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations#create-a-routine) in the official docs. For what it stops to ask you about, see [Approvals and privacy](/en/howto/approvals-privacy/).

Get the must-haves straight, and your time goes only to the people worth meeting.

## Stuck?

<Q q="It can't open a resume attachment.">
It may not read encrypted, password-protected, or unusual files. Ask the applicant to send a PDF instead.
</Q>
<Q q="It booked an interview over something else.">
The Google Calendar app sees only the calendars your account can see. If your plans live in several calendars, tell it which ones to check.
</Q>
<Q q="I want applicants to email the bot directly.">
Give the bot its own email address, then use a plus alias like `yourname+jobs@mail.grokbot.com`. A routine that watches the alias runs only for mail sent to that alias. Anyone can write to an alias, though, so the bot still screens everyone against the must-haves.
</Q>
