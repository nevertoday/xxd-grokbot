Monday morning, you open your inbox to two hundred unread emails. You click through them one by one, and most are ads and notifications, while the customer who asked for a quote on Friday afternoon sits at the very bottom. By the time you get there, they've waited all weekend. It's the same if you freelance or you're job hunting: a client's change request or an interview invite can get buried under a pile of alerts. When you finish this page, your bot will sort the night's email every weekday morning into three piles, urgent, needs a reply, and FYI, label each one, and have reply drafts ready for the urgent ones. All you do is read them and send.

The hard part is teaching it what "urgent" means. <mark>Sort "urgent" by what happens if you wait. It's urgent only if not replying today would hold something up.</mark> Skip that, and it flags everything as urgent, which is the same as not sorting at all.

The example below follows someone with an office job. The official docs call this an Inbox manager. See [Use cases](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/use-cases#inbox-manager).

## The goal isn't inbox zero

Inbox zero is just a means. What you want is a same-day reply to the email that matters, and the rest not eating your time. Make a new <Term k="bot" />, call it Potato, and vent a little:

<Copy id="ib-goal" title="First message: the goal" code="Hey Potato. I spend an hour every morning just sorting email, and the important stuff still gets buried. Last time a customer asked for a quote, I didn't see it for two days. I want you looking after my inbox: the important email gets answered the same day, and the rest stops eating my time, so I can start on real work first thing. Keep this in mind, because it's an every-morning thing for you now." />

For a new name that fits the job, add one more line at the end. [Beginner lesson 2](/en/start/2-first-bot/) has it.

## Tell it how you sort your mail each morning

Now tell it how you handle your inbox today: whose mail you open first, how you sort it, and what you check before you reply. Here's what this office worker might say. Swap in your own habits:

<Copy id="ib-process" title="Second message: your process" code={`When I open my email in the morning, I go for customers and my manager first, then anything with a deadline, plus invoices and contracts. Most of what's left is ads and system notifications, and I skip those from the subject line. In my head there are three piles, urgent, needs a reply, and FYI. I label each one to match and answer the urgent ones first. If a reply needs to offer a time, I check my calendar for when I'm free. I read every draft myself before it goes out, and I'll tell you when you put something in the wrong pile and what I changed in a draft.

Plan it as if I'll never sort my mail again. Don't label anything yet. Just tell me how you'd sort it.`} />

The app it connects is Gmail, and it checks Google Calendar when it offers a time. The first time it needs one, a **Connect** card shows up in the chat. Open it and sign in. Where its sorting differs from yours, point it out, and keep going until you're happy.

## What counts as "urgent"

You can tell from a subject line whether an email is urgent, because you know what happens if you don't answer. The bot doesn't, so tell it the rules you go by:

<Copy id="ib-judge" title="Third message: how you decide" code={`Sort by these. It's urgent only if not replying today would hold something up, and ads and system notifications never are. Write drafts the way I talk, short and to the point. Anywhere I have to decide something, like a price or a date, leave a gap and point it out. In a thread, only reply to the latest message.

I probably haven't named everyone I answer right away. Look at the emails I've replied to fastest lately, and ask me about the ones you're unsure of.`} />

The first rule ties "urgent" to consequences, so it stops flagging everything. The second means it never agrees to anything for you. Prices and dates you have to decide stay blank, and the blanks show you exactly what to fill in. The third keeps it to one reply per thread, so nobody gets three answers.

What "urgent" looks like is different for everyone. If you freelance, it might be the revision a client is waiting on. If you're job hunting, it's interview invites and emails asking you to send more documents. Just rewrite the first rule with your own consequences.

Then test it on the customer email you forgot to answer. Which pile would it go in, and what would it write? Until the rules settle, don't set it to run on its own. To have it ask about everything you didn't cover, use [this prompt on the prompts page](/en/howto/prompts/#p-gaps).

## Every weekday at 7 AM, it sorts for you

Once it has sorted a few mornings and you've barely changed anything, let it do this every day. First save it as a <Term k="技能" />, written instructions it follows every time, then set up a <Term k="routine" /> that says when to start:

<Copy id="ib-routine" title="Fourth message: let it start on its own" code={`Turn the way we settled on into a skill called "Morning inbox," with a routine that runs it every weekday at 7:00 AM on the email that came in overnight. Every reply stays a draft, and not one gets sent. If my email won't connect, tell me in this chat.`} />

Once it's running, this is roughly what you'll see in the chat each morning:

<Reply code={`All sorted: 3 urgent, 12 need a reply, and the rest are notifications. They're all labeled. I've drafted replies to the 3 urgent ones. In the quote request from the customer, I left the price blank. Have a look and change anything you want.`} />

Emails it prepares are draft cards by default, marked **Needs approval**. Check the recipients and the body, then choose **Send**. To drop one, choose **Discard**.

After the first few runs, pick a few emails and see which pile and label they got, then look in your Gmail Sent folder, which should have nothing from it. If the drafts don't sound like you, rewrite one yourself and show it.

Set it up in the evening, and it first runs at 7:00 AM the next weekday. To watch it sort right now, open it and choose **Test**. A test really labels your email, and the replies are still only drafts. See [Create a routine](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations#create-a-routine) in the official docs. For what it stops to ask you about, see [Approvals and privacy](/en/howto/approvals-privacy/).

Explain "urgent" once, and from then on it sorts by that rule every morning.

## Stuck?

<Q q="The drafts have no sign-off.">
Gmail adds your signature under the draft, so the bot leaves the sign-off to it. The email goes out with your usual signature.
</Q>
<Q q="It's sorting the wrong mailbox.">
Gmail connects one mailbox at a time. Open **Connect apps**, disconnect the current one, and authorize the one you want.
</Q>
<Q q="An email tells it to transfer money.">
Grok Bot's Auto Review checks each action before the bot takes it. It counts what you ask for in your own messages, and text in someone else's email doesn't count as your request. To handle every payment yourself, don't save a card in <Term k="Vault" />, because a saved card fills in at checkout without asking you. See [Approvals and privacy](/en/howto/approvals-privacy/).
</Q>
