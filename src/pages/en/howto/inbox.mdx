Monday morning, you open your inbox to two hundred unread emails. You click through them one by one, and most are ads and notifications, while the customer who asked for a quote on Friday afternoon sits at the very bottom. By the time you get there, they've waited all weekend. It's the same if you freelance or you're job hunting: a client's change request or an interview invite can get buried under a pile of alerts. When you finish this page, your bot will sort the night's email every weekday morning into three piles, urgent, needs a reply, and FYI, label each one, and have reply drafts ready for the urgent ones. All you do is read them and send.

The hard part is teaching it what "urgent" means. <mark>Sort "urgent" by what happens if you wait. It's urgent only if not replying today would hold something up.</mark> Skip that, and it flags everything as urgent, which is the same as not sorting at all.

The example below follows someone with an office job. It takes four steps: give it the goal, explain how you do it now, teach it how you decide, then let it start on its own. The official docs call this an Inbox manager. See [Use cases](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/use-cases#inbox-manager).

## The goal isn't inbox zero

Getting to inbox zero is only one step in the process. What you actually want is a same-day reply to the email that matters, and the rest not eating your time. Make a new <Term k="bot" />, call it Potato, and tell it where you're at and what you want:

<Copy id="ib-goal" title="First message: the goal" code="Hi Potato. I spend an hour every morning just sorting email, and the important stuff still gets buried. Last time a customer asked for a quote, I didn't see it for two days. I'd like you to take this over: get the important email answered the same day, and keep the rest from eating my time. That way I can start on real work first thing. This is your job from now on, so remember it. Then pick a name and a picture for yourself that fit the job." />

## Tell it how you sort your mail each morning

Now tell it how you handle your inbox today: the piles you use, the people you watch for, and what you check before you reply. Here's an example. Swap in your own habits:

<Copy id="ib-process" title="Second message: your process" code={`Potato, here's how I do it now. Each morning I sort the unread email into three piles: urgent, needs a reply, and FYI. I label each one to match, then write replies to the urgent ones. I watch hardest for email from customers and my manager, anything with a deadline, invoices, and contracts. I use Gmail, and when a reply needs to offer a time, I check Google Calendar for when I'm free. I read every draft before it goes out. I'll tell you when you put something in the wrong pile and what I changed in a draft.

Think about how you could take this off my plate completely. Before you start, tell me back in plain words what you think the goal is and how you'd go about it, and I'll tell you if you've got it. Don't do anything yet. Let's get the plan right first.`} />

If typing is slow, press `Cmd/Ctrl+D` and just talk, saying things as they come to you. It restates what it heard first. Correct whatever's off and go a few rounds until it's right. When you're happy, tell it to go ahead. If it needs an app, it shows a **Connect** card in the chat. Open it and sign in.

## What counts as "urgent"

You can tell from a subject line whether an email is urgent, because you know what happens if you don't answer. The bot doesn't, so tell it the rules you go by:

<Copy id="ib-judge" title="Third message: how you decide" code={`Potato, here are a few rules I go by. It's urgent only if not replying today would hold something up. Ads and system notifications are never urgent. Write drafts the way I talk, short and to the point. Anywhere I have to decide something, like a price or a date, leave it blank and flag it for me. In a thread, only reply to the latest message.

Also, help me figure out what I haven't told you yet: how I make calls, where my info comes from, how I know it's done right, and when you should come to me. One question at a time. Then walk me through a few examples and I'll tell you what's off. Don't set anything to run on its own yet.`} />

The first rule ties "urgent" to consequences, so it stops flagging everything. The second means it never agrees to anything for you. Prices and dates you have to decide stay blank, and the blanks show you exactly what to fill in. The third keeps it to one reply per thread, so nobody gets three answers.

What "urgent" looks like is different for everyone. If you freelance, it might be the revision a client is waiting on. If you're job hunting, it's interview invites and emails asking you to send more documents. Just rewrite the first rule with your own consequences.

When it's done asking, try it on a real email or two that went wrong, like a customer email you forgot to answer. See how it would handle them, then tell it what to fix.

## Every weekday at 7 AM, it sorts for you

Once this has gone well a few times, you can let it start on its own. First have it save the process as a <Term k="技能" />, written instructions it follows every time, then set up a <Term k="routine" /> that tells it when to start:

<Copy id="ib-routine" title="Fourth message: let it start on its own" code={`Save what we just worked out as a skill called "Morning inbox." Then set up a routine: every weekday at 7:00 AM, use that skill on the email that came in overnight. Write every reply as a draft, and don't send a single one. If Gmail won't connect, tell me in this chat.`} />

Once it's running, this is roughly what you'll see in the chat each morning:

<Reply code={`All sorted: 3 urgent, 12 need a reply, and the rest are notifications. They're all labeled. I've drafted replies to the 3 urgent ones. In the quote request from the customer, I left the price blank. Have a look and change anything you want.`} />

Emails it prepares are draft cards by default, marked **Needs approval**. Check the recipients and the body, then choose **Send**. To drop one, choose **Discard**.

After the first few runs, pick a few emails and see which pile and label they got, then look in your Gmail Sent folder, which should have nothing from it. If the drafts don't sound like you, rewrite one yourself and show it.

A new routine waits for its next scheduled time. If you'd rather not wait, open it and choose **Test**, but keep in mind that a test does real work. See [Create a routine](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations#create-a-routine) in the official docs. For what it stops to ask you about, see [Approvals and privacy](/en/howto/approvals-privacy/).

Explain "urgent" once, and from then on it sorts by that rule every morning.

## Stuck?

<Q q="The drafts have no sign-off.">
Gmail adds your signature under the draft, so the bot leaves the sign-off to it. The email goes out with your usual signature.
</Q>
<Q q="It's sorting the wrong mailbox.">
Gmail connects one mailbox at a time. Open **Connect apps**, disconnect the current one, and authorize the one you want.
</Q>
<Q q="An email tells it to transfer money.">
Grok Bot's Auto Review checks each action before the bot takes it. It counts what you ask for in your own messages, and text in someone else's email doesn't count as your request. To handle every payment yourself, don't save a card in <Term k="Vault" />, because a saved card fills in at checkout without asking you. See [Approvals and privacy](/en/howto/approvals-privacy/).
</Q>
