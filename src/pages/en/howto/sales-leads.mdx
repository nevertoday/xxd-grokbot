By the end of this page, you'll open your lead sheet each weekday morning and find every lead since yesterday already scored. For the good fits, replies offering meeting times are drafted and waiting for you to read and send.

Inquiries from your website and email go cold fast. Reply slowly and the customer goes somewhere else. But each morning you have to read them one by one, look up each company, and check your calendar before you can answer, and by the time you're done the best ones may have moved on. Replying to inquiries is only one step in the process. What you want is for good-fit customers to get a meeting soon, so you close more deals. <mark>Before a bot can screen for you, you have to turn "good fit" into a few things it can check, so you can read its scores.</mark>

## The goal is a meeting, not a reply

The official docs call this Sales development. See [Use cases](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/use-cases#sales-outbound).

Make a new <Term k="bot" />, call it Potato, and send it the goal:

<Copy id="sl-goal" title="First message: the goal" code="Hi Potato. I want every good-fit customer who reaches out to get a meeting soon, so that we close more deals. This is your job from now on, so remember it. Based on the goal, choose a name and a picture for yourself." />

## Tell it how you screen each morning

Next, tell it how you do this today. Pressing `Cmd/Ctrl+D` and talking is the easiest way, saying things as they come to you. Here's an example. Swap in your own criteria and tools:

<Copy id="sl-process" title="Second message: your process" code={`Potato, here's how I usually do this myself. Each morning I read the new inquiries and score them against our ideal customer. Good fits get a reply offering times, and the rest get a polite no. If someone doesn't answer, I follow up once after three days. I watch for a few things: company size, budget, and whether it's a competitor fishing for information. I use Gmail and Google Calendar, and I track leads in a Google Sheets list. The information comes from emails sent by our website form, and from the lead's company website. How do I know it's right? Every lead in the sheet has a status, and the times in each reply are really free on my calendar. For feedback, I look at which leads turned into deals.

Think about how you could take this over from me completely. Before you begin, restate my goal and process in simple words, so I can give you feedback and confirm it. Don't start doing the work yet. Let's get the process planned first.`} />

It restates what it heard first. Correct anything that's off and go a few rounds, then tell it to go ahead when you're happy. If it needs an app, it shows a **Connect** card in the chat. Open it and sign in.

## Turn "good fit" into something it can check

You can tell in seconds whether an inquiry is worth answering, because you carry a yardstick in your head. Your bot doesn't have one, so it would have to guess, and you couldn't tell how it got its scores. So write the yardstick down:

<Copy id="sl-judge" title="Third message: how you decide" code={`Potato, here are a few rules I use to decide. A good fit comes down to three things: industry, headcount, and budget. Missing one drops it a level. If there isn't enough to judge, check their company website first. If that doesn't settle it, mark it "unsure" and hand it to me. Follow up at most twice. After two follow-ups with no answer, stop and leave them alone.

Then help me find what I haven't told you about this process yet. Ask me how I make decisions, where I get my information, how I check the work, and when you should come to me. Ask one question at a time. Then walk me through a few examples so I can give you feedback. Don't automate anything yet.`} />

This way it looks things up before it asks, so you only get the truly unclear ones, and with a limit on follow-ups nobody gets chased. When you pick examples, include one or two that went wrong, like the lead you almost turned away that became a big deal. See how it would handle them, then tell it what to fix.

## Let it screen first thing every morning

Once this has gone well a few times, it can start on its own. Save the process as a <Term k="技能" /> first, then set up a <Term k="routine" /> so it starts on time every weekday morning:

<Copy id="sl-routine" title="Fourth message: let it start on its own" code={`Save the process we just worked out as a skill called "Lead screening." Then set up a routine: every weekday at 9:00 AM, use that skill on leads that came in since yesterday, and update the lead sheet. Write every reply as a draft for me to send. Don't contact any customer.`} />

After the first few runs, check its work. Each lead should note which things it checked, so every score has reasons. Compare the offered times with your calendar to see that they're really free. And every reply should still be a draft, with nothing sent.

A new routine waits for its next scheduled time. To try it now, open it and choose **Test**, but know that a test does real work. See [Create a routine](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations#create-a-routine) in the official docs.

The official docs suggest keeping sending behind an approval. To see what it stops to ask you about first, read [Approvals and privacy](/en/howto/approvals-privacy/).

## Stuck?

<Q q="It offered a customer a time when I'm busy.">
The Google Calendar app sees only the calendars your account can see. If your plans live in several calendars, tell it which ones to check.
</Q>
<Q q="Our customer list lives in a system it can't connect to.">
For a site with no app, it uses the browser on its <Term k="云端电脑" />. Save the login in <Term k="Vault" />, and it fills it in without seeing the password.
</Q>
<Q q="I want it to email a lot of people at once.">
When it sends from the bot's own email address, a first-contact email goes to one person per message and includes a one-click unsubscribe link. There's also a limit on how many new people it can email. See [Give your bots an email address](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/computer-and-apps#give-your-bots-an-email-address) in the official docs.
</Q>
