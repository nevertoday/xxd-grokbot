## Say what result you want

Replying to every inquiry is one step in the process. The result you want is that good-fit customers get a meeting soon, and you close more deals.

The official docs call this Sales development. See [Use cases](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/use-cases#sales-outbound).

Make a new <Term k="bot" />, call it Potato, and send it the goal:

<Copy id="sl-goal" title="First message: the goal" code="Hi Potato. I want you to own this goal: every good-fit customer who reaches out gets a meeting soon, so we close more deals. This is your job from now on, so remember it. Based on the goal, choose a name and a picture for yourself." />

## Tell it how you do it now

Tell it how you do this today. Change every line to fit you:

<Copy id="sl-process" title="Second message: your process" code={`Potato, this is how I usually do this myself:
- Steps: each morning I read new inquiries and score them against our ideal customer. Good fits get a reply offering times. The rest get a polite no. If someone doesn't answer, I follow up once after three days.
- What I watch for: company size, budget, and whether it's a competitor fishing for information.
- Apps I use: Gmail and Google Calendar, with leads tracked in a Google Sheets list.
- How I check it's right: every lead in the sheet has a status. The times in each reply are really free on my calendar.
- Where the information comes from: emails from our website form, and the lead's company website.
- Where feedback comes from: which leads turned into deals.

Think about how you could take this over from me completely. Before you begin, restate my goal and process in simple words, so I can give you feedback and confirm it. Don't start doing the work yet. Let's get the process planned first.`} />

If typing is slow, press `Cmd/Ctrl+D` and talk. Say things as they come to you. It restates what it heard first. Correct anything that's off, and go a few rounds.

When you're happy, tell it to go ahead. If it needs an app, it shows a **Connect** card in the chat. Open it and sign in.

## Teach it how you decide

You make some calls without thinking, and your bot can't know them. Here are three common ones for this job:

<Copy id="sl-judge" title="Third message: how you decide" code={`Potato, here are a few rules I use to decide:
1. A good fit comes down to three things: industry, headcount, and budget. Missing one drops it a level.
2. If there isn't enough to judge, check their company website first. If that doesn't settle it, mark it "unsure" and hand it to me.
3. Follow up at most twice. After two follow-ups with no answer, stop.

Help me find what I haven't told you about this process yet. Ask me how I make decisions, where I get my information, how I check the work, and when you should ask me for help. Ask one question at a time. Then walk me through a few examples so I can give you feedback. Don't automate anything yet.`} />

- **Rule 1.** <mark>Turn "good fit" into things you can check, so you can read its scores.</mark>
- **Rule 2.** It looks things up before asking, so you only get the truly unclear ones.
- **Rule 3.** Follow-ups have a limit, so nobody gets chased.

Include an example or two that went wrong, like the lead you almost turned away that became a big deal. See how it would handle them, then tell it what to fix.

## Make it run on its own

Once this has gone well a few times, let it start on its own. Save the process as a <Term k="技能" /> first, then set up a <Term k="routine" />:

<Copy id="sl-routine" title="Fourth message: let it start on its own" code={`Save the process we just used as a skill called "Lead screening."
Then set up a routine: every weekday at 9:00 AM, use that skill on leads that came in since yesterday, and update the lead sheet.
Write every reply as a draft for me to send.
Don't contact any customer.`} />

After the first few runs, check these:

- **Each score has reasons.** Every lead notes which things it checked.
- **The offered times are free.** Compare them with your calendar.
- **Nothing was sent.** Replies are still drafts.

A new routine waits for its next scheduled time. To try it now, open it and choose **Test**. A test does real work. See [Create a routine](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations#create-a-routine) in the official docs.

The official docs suggest keeping sending behind an approval. To see what it asks you about first, read [Approvals and privacy](/en/howto/approvals-privacy/).

## Stuck?

<Q q="It offered a customer a time when I'm busy.">
The Google Calendar app sees only the calendars your account can see. If your plans live in several calendars, tell it which ones to check.
</Q>
<Q q="Our customer list lives in a system it can't connect to.">
For a site with no app, it uses the browser on its <Term k="云端电脑" />. Save the login in <Term k="Vault" />, and it fills it in without seeing the password.
</Q>
<Q q="I want it to email a lot of people at once.">
When it sends from the bot's own email address, a first-contact email goes to one person per message and includes a one-click unsubscribe link. There's also a limit on how many new people it can email. See [Give your bots an email address](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/computer-and-apps#give-your-bots-an-email-address) in the official docs.
</Q>
