## Say what result you want

Booking a flight is one step in the process. The result you want is that once a trip is set, tickets and a hotel are sorted fast, and all you do is choose.

The official docs call this a Travel coordinator. See [Use cases](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/use-cases#travel-coordinator).

Make a new <Term k="bot" />, call it Potato, and send it the goal:

<Copy id="tr-goal" title="First message: the goal" code="Hi Potato. I want you to own this goal: for every trip, you compare flights and hotels, and all I do is choose. I don't want to search websites myself. This is your job from now on, so remember it. Based on the goal, choose a name and a picture for yourself." />

## Tell it how you do it now

Tell it how you do this today. Change every line to fit you:

<Copy id="tr-process" title="Second message: your process" code={`Potato, this is how I usually do this myself:
- Steps: I check my calendar for the meeting I'm traveling to, look up flights, and compare times and prices. Then I find a hotel near where the meeting is. Give me two options. I book only after I choose, and then it goes on my calendar.
- What I watch for: no red-eyes, at least 2 hours for a connection, and free changes or cancellation when possible.
- Apps I use: Google Calendar and Gmail. For airline and hotel websites, use your own browser.
- How I check it's right: each option has links and a total price, and I see the same price when I open them.
- Where the information comes from: meetings on my calendar and invitations in my email.
- Where feedback comes from: which option I picked, and why I didn't pick the other.

Think about how you could take this over from me completely. Before you begin, restate my goal and process in simple words, so I can give you feedback and confirm it. Don't start doing the work yet. Let's get the process planned first.`} />

If typing is slow, press `Cmd/Ctrl+D` and talk. Say things as they come to you. It restates what it heard first. Correct anything that's off, and go a few rounds.

When you're happy, tell it to go ahead. If it needs an app, it shows a **Connect** card in the chat. Open it and sign in.

## Teach it how you decide

You make some calls without thinking, and your bot can't know them. Here are three common ones for this job:

<Copy id="tr-judge" title="Third message: how you decide" code={`Potato, here are a few rules I use to decide:
1. If a nonstop costs less than $75 more than a connection, take the nonstop.
2. My calendar is right about meeting times. If an email says something different, ask me first.
3. Booking isn't done when you pay. It's done when the confirmation email is in and the trip is on my calendar.

Help me find what I haven't told you about this process yet. Ask me how I make decisions, where I get my information, how I check the work, and when you should ask me for help. Ask one question at a time. Then walk me through a few examples so I can give you feedback. Don't automate anything yet.`} />

- **Rule 1.** When cheap and fast pull apart, it knows your trade-off.
- **Rule 2.** When the two disagree, it knows which one to trust.
- **Rule 3.** With the confirmation and the calendar entry in place, you can stop thinking about the trip.

Include an example or two that went wrong, like the connection you missed. See how it would handle them, then tell it what to fix.

<Callout type="warn" title="The payment step">
A card saved in <Term k="Vault" /> fills in at checkout without asking you each time. <mark>To approve every payment yourself, don't save the card.</mark> It asks for the card with a Secure Form, which fills the page without showing the bot what you typed. In that form, leave **Securely save to Bot's vault** unchecked. <u>A form without that checkbox saves the card straight to Vault, so don't submit it.</u> See [Logins and cards go in Vault](/en/howto/vault-logins/).
</Callout>

## Make it run on its own

Once this has gone well a few times, let it start on its own. Save the process as a <Term k="技能" /> first, then set up a <Term k="routine" />.

This job starts when an email arrives. You forward a trip notice to the bot's email address, and it starts looking. If it doesn't have an address yet, tell it "Get yourself an email address." It asks what name you want, and nothing is claimed until you approve. On a Cursor team, an admin has to turn this on first. <u>You get one address per account, and you can't rename or delete it.</u>

<Copy id="tr-routine" title="Fourth message: let it start on its own" code={`Save the process we just used as a skill called "Trip planning."
Then set up a routine: when an email I forward from my own address arrives in your inbox, use that skill to find two options and post them in this chat.
Don't book anything, and don't pay for anything.
If nothing fits, tell me. Don't loosen my rules.`} />

After the first few runs, check these:

- **It didn't book.** The options are in the chat, and there's no booking confirmation in your email.
- **The prices match.** Open the links and check the totals.
- **Your rules held.** No red-eyes, and connections have enough time.

To try it now, forward an old trip notice to it. See [Trigger work from an event](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations#trigger-work-from-an-event) in the official docs.

To see what it asks you about first, read [Approvals and privacy](/en/howto/approvals-privacy/).

## Stuck?

<Q q="A website wants me to sign in or enter a code.">
For a login, it tries Vault first, then a connected 1Password, then a Secure Form for you to fill in. It can read a code a site emails you from a connected Gmail. When none of that works, like a QR code, it hands you the computer. Choose **Take over**, finish that step, and choose **I'm done**. Then tell it to continue. Never paste a password or a code into the chat.
</Q>
<Q q="One website never loads.">
Some sites drop connections from the bot's computer. Open **Settings → Computer**, and under **Your computer** turn on **Route traffic through this computer**. While the desktop app is open, the site sees your own network.
</Q>
<Q q="An email arrived, but it didn't start.">
It counts only new email. Mail from before the routine existed doesn't start a run. Also check the sender in the routine. It should be the address you forward from.
</Q>
