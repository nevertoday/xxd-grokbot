A relative is getting married in another city next month, and the four of you are going for three days. You've got a dozen tabs open comparing flights and hotels. This one's cheap but has a connection, that one's nonstop but lands after midnight, and the hotel needs room for four. By the time you've compared everything, the flight you liked has gone up. When you finish this page, you'll forward the invitation email to your bot's address once the dates are set, and it will compare flights and hotels and post two options in the chat, each with links and a total price. A work trip or a trip home for the holidays works the same way with a few different rules.

<mark>Searching and comparing go to the bot. Which option to take, and whether to pay, stay with you.</mark> The rules and the payment note below show where that line goes.

It takes four steps: give it the goal, explain how you do it now, teach it how you decide, then let it start on its own. The official docs call this a Travel coordinator. See [Use cases](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/use-cases#travel-coordinator).

## The goal isn't a plane ticket

Booking a flight is only one step in the process. What you actually want is that once the dates are set, tickets and a hotel get sorted fast, and all you do is choose. Make a new <Term k="bot" />, call it Potato, and tell it where you're at and what you want:

<Copy id="tr-goal" title="First message: the goal" code="Hi Potato. Every time I take the family somewhere, I end up with a dozen tabs open comparing flights and hotels, then checking times against my calendar, and there goes the whole evening. I'd like you to take this over: once the dates are set, compare flights and hotels and bring me two options, and I'll just pick one. That way I'm not trawling websites one by one anymore. This is your job from now on, so remember it. Then pick a name and a picture for yourself that fit the job." />

## Tell it how you pick flights and hotels

Now tell it how you plan a trip today: what you look at first, what you compare, and what you won't accept. Here's an example. Swap in your own family's details:

<Copy id="tr-process" title="Second message: your process" code={`Potato, here's how I do it now. First I check my calendar for anything else on those days, then I look up flights and compare times and prices, then I find a hotel close to where we're going. There are four of us and the kids are little, so no red-eyes, at least 2 hours for any connection, a hotel room that fits four, and free changes or cancellation when possible. The dates are in Google Calendar, and the invitation and emails from family are in Gmail. For airline and hotel websites, use your own browser. Bring me two options. I only book after I pick one, and then it goes on my calendar. Each option needs links and a total price, and it's right when I open the link and see the same price. I'll tell you which one I picked, and why I didn't pick the other.

Think about how you could take this off my plate completely. Before you start, tell me back in plain words what you think the goal is and how you'd go about it, and I'll tell you if you've got it. Don't do anything yet. Let's get the plan right first.`} />

If typing is slow, press `Cmd/Ctrl+D` and just talk, saying things as they come to you. It restates what it heard first. Correct whatever's off and go a few rounds until it's right. When you're happy, tell it to go ahead. If it needs an app, it shows a **Connect** card in the chat. Open it and sign in.

## When cheap and fast pull apart

When you book, you keep a running tally in your head: how much extra a nonstop is worth, which to trust when an email and your calendar disagree, and when a booking is really done. The bot knows none of this, so tell it:

<Copy id="tr-judge" title="Third message: how you decide" code={`Potato, here are a few rules I go by. If a nonstop costs only a little more, less than 10% of the total, take the nonstop. Connections with kids are exhausting. The dates on my calendar are the right ones. If an email says something different, ask me first. Booking isn't done when you pay. It's done when the confirmation email is in and the trip is on my calendar.

Also, help me figure out what I haven't told you yet: how I make calls, where my info comes from, how I know it's done right, and when you should come to me. One question at a time. Then walk me through a few examples and I'll tell you what's off. Don't set anything to run on its own yet.`} />

The first rule tells it how you trade money against time, and the second tells it which source to trust when the two disagree. The third puts "done" at the moment the confirmation and the calendar entry are both in place, because that's when you can really stop thinking about the trip.

The rules change with the person. For a work trip, the hotel should be close to the meeting, and the meeting invite has the right dates. A student going home for the break may only care about price, so the rule might be "cheapest first, one connection is fine."

When it's done asking, try it on a real trip or two that went wrong, like the connection you missed and the night you spent at the airport with the kids. See how it would handle them, then tell it what to fix.

<Callout type="warn" title="The payment step">
A card saved in <Term k="Vault" /> fills in at checkout without asking you each time. So if you want to approve every payment yourself, don't save the card. When it needs a card, it asks with a Secure Form, which fills the page without showing the bot what you typed. In that form, leave **Securely save to Bot's vault** unchecked. <u>A form without that checkbox saves the card straight to Vault, so don't submit it.</u> See [Logins and cards go in Vault](/en/howto/vault-logins/).
</Callout>

## Forward an email, and it gets to work

Once this has gone well a few times, you can let it start on its own. As before, have it save the process as a <Term k="技能" />, then set up a <Term k="routine" />. This one doesn't run on a clock, though. It waits for an email: once the dates are set, you forward the invitation or whatever email the trip came from to the bot's address, and it starts looking. If there's no email to forward, write a couple of lines from your own address instead, like "12th to 15th, four of us, going to a wedding."

If it doesn't have an address yet, tell it "Get yourself an email address." It asks what name you want, and nothing is claimed until you approve. On a Cursor team, an admin has to turn this on first. <u>You get one address per account, and you can't rename or delete it</u>, so settle on the name before you approve.

<Copy id="tr-routine" title="Fourth message: let it start on its own" code={`Save what we just worked out as a skill called "Trip planning." Then set up a routine: whenever an email from my own address lands in your inbox, forwarded or written by me, use that skill to find two options and post them in this chat. Don't book anything, and don't pay for anything. If nothing fits, just tell me. Don't loosen my rules on your own.`} />

After the first few runs, first make sure it didn't book: the options are in the chat, and there's no booking confirmation in your email. Then open the links and check the totals. Last, check that your rules held, with no red-eyes and enough time for every connection.

To try it now, forward it an old invitation or trip email. See [Trigger work from an event](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations#trigger-work-from-an-event) in the official docs. For what it stops to ask you about, see [Approvals and privacy](/en/howto/approvals-privacy/).

From here on, the bot does the website hopping. You read two options and pick one.

## Stuck?

<Q q="A website wants me to sign in or enter a code.">
For a login, it tries Vault first, then a connected 1Password, then a Secure Form for you to fill in. It can read a code a site emails you from a connected Gmail. Only when none of that works, like with a QR code, does it hand you the computer. Choose **Take over**, finish that step, and choose **I'm done**. Then tell it to continue. Never paste a password or a code into the chat.
</Q>
<Q q="One website never loads.">
Some sites drop connections from the bot's computer. Open **Settings → Computer**, and under **Your computer** turn on **Route traffic through this computer**. Then, while the desktop app is open, the site sees your own network.
</Q>
<Q q="An email arrived, but it didn't start.">
It counts only new email, so mail from before the routine existed doesn't start a run. Also check the sender in the routine. It should be the address you forward from.
</Q>
