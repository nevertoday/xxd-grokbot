If you're a student, your deadlines are scattered across school email and the websites for each of your classes, and it's 11 PM before you notice a report is due in the morning. Office work isn't much different. You sit down and go through your email, your calendar, and Slack one after another just to find out what your day holds, and the one email that mattered may be buried under a pile of cc's. When you finish this page, your bot will post a short list in the chat every weekday at 7:30 AM with only the things that need you today and over the next few days, each with a link to the original email or page.

What makes that list useful isn't how much it covers. It's whether the bot knows what "needs you" means. <mark>Spell out what "needs you" means, and the briefing stays short.</mark>

The example below follows a student. If you work in an office, you only need to swap a few things, and the page points them out as it goes. It takes four steps: give it the goal, explain how you do it now, teach it how you decide, then let it start on its own. The official docs call this a Morning briefing. See [Use cases](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/use-cases#chief-of-staff).

## The goal isn't a briefing

Writing a briefing is only one step in the process. What you actually want is to know first thing which things today can't happen without you, so you're not scrambling the night before a deadline. So make a new <Term k="bot" />, call it Potato, and tell it where you're at and what you want:

<Copy id="mb-goal" title="First message: the goal" code="Hi Potato. Every morning I go through my school email, then click into the website for each of my classes, just to figure out what's due. More than once I've only remembered the night before and stayed up half the night finishing it. I'd like you to take this over: before class each day, tell me what I need to do myself today and over the next few days. That way I'm not up at 2 AM anymore. This is your job from now on, so remember it. Then pick a name and a picture for yourself that fit the job." />

## Walk it through your morning rounds

Now tell it how you do this today. Don't worry about saying too much. It needs to know where you look first, where you look next, and what you keep an eye out for. Talk the way you'd explain it to a person. Here's what that student might say. Swap in your own places and habits:

<Copy id="mb-process" title="Second message: your process" code={`Potato, here's how I do it now. First thing, I check my school email, because that's where teachers announce new deadlines and room changes. Then I open each class website and look for new assignments and announcements. Last, I check Google Calendar for today's classes and any group meetings. The three things I'm scared of missing are anything due today, any test this week, and anything a teacher changed at the last minute. Every item on the list should link to the email or the class page it came from, and it's right when I open the link and it matches. After I read it each day, I'll tell you what was extra and what was missing.

Think about how you could take this off my plate completely. Before you start, tell me back in plain words what you think the goal is and how you'd go about it, and I'll tell you if you've got it. Don't do anything yet. Let's get the plan right first.`} />

Class websites don't have an app, so it opens them in the browser on its own computer. If you work in an office, swap the class websites for your Slack mentions and the Google Drive docs that changed since yesterday, so the apps are Gmail, Google Calendar, Slack, and Google Drive. What you're scared of missing becomes calendar clashes, anything due today, and messages from your manager or customers.

If typing is slow, press `Cmd/Ctrl+D` and just talk, saying things as they come to you. It restates what it heard first. Correct whatever's off and go a few rounds until it's right. When you're happy, tell it to go ahead. If it needs an app, it shows a **Connect** card in the chat. Open it and sign in.

## What counts as "needs me"

When you go through it yourself, you can tell at a glance what needs doing and what's just FYI. But you've never said those calls out loud, so the bot has no way to know them. Send one more message with the rules you go by:

<Copy id="mb-judge" title="Third message: how you decide" code={`Potato, here are a few rules I go by. Anything due today, anything due in the next three days that I haven't turned in, and anything a teacher moved to a new time or place counts as "needs me." Mass emails about clubs and talks are "good to know," so put them in one line at the bottom. If yesterday's list already had something and nothing's changed, leave it out. And if you're not sure whether something needs me, put it at the top and ask me. Don't just drop it.

Also, help me figure out what I haven't told you yet: how I make calls, where my info comes from, how I know it's done right, and when you should come to me. One question at a time. Then walk me through a few examples and I'll tell you what's off. Don't set anything to run on its own yet.`} />

The first rule matters most, because once "needs me" has clear edges, the list gets short on its own. The second means you only read what's new each day. The third might look fussy, but missing one thing costs more than reading one extra line, so anything unclear goes to you. If you work in an office, make the first rule "someone's waiting on my reply, I have to make a call today, or there's a calendar clash," and emails where you're only cc'd count as good to know.

When it's done asking, try it on a real case or two that went wrong, like the report you almost forgot to hand in. See how it would handle them, then tell it what to fix.

## Every weekday at 7:30, it shows up on its own

Once this has gone well a few times, you can let it start on its own. First have it save the process as a <Term k="技能" />, written instructions it follows every time, then set up a <Term k="routine" /> that tells it when to start:

<Copy id="mb-routine" title="Fourth message: let it start on its own" code={`Save what we just worked out as a skill called "Morning briefing." Then set up a routine: every weekday at 7:30 AM, use that skill to make the list and post it in this chat, and nowhere else. If you can't open or read one of the places, say so at the top of the list. Don't fill the gap with old stuff.`} />

Once it's running, this is roughly what you'll see each morning:

<Reply code={`One question first: the library emailed that your book is due back the day after tomorrow. Does that count as needing you?

Needs you today and this week:
1. Stats homework is due tonight at 11, and you haven't turned it in. (class site)
2. Thursday's group meeting moved to 4 PM. Your teacher emailed last night. (email)
3. Midterm is next Tuesday, and the review topics just went up. (class site)

Good to know: sign-up for the guest talk closes next week.`} />

After the first few runs, open the routine and check that **When to run** shows the time you meant. The time zone is under **Settings**, at **Timezone**. Then open two or three links and check them against the original. If a place couldn't be read, the top of the list should say so.

A new routine waits for its next scheduled time. If you'd rather not wait, open it and choose **Test**, but keep in mind that a test does real work. See [Create a routine](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations#create-a-routine) in the official docs.

If you work in an office and later want the briefing posted to a team channel, have it write a draft first, and send it after you've read it. For what it stops to ask you about, see [Approvals and privacy](/en/howto/approvals-privacy/).

Spend a few rounds up front on what "needs you" means, and from then on your mornings start with one short list.

## Stuck?

<Q q="The briefing didn't arrive on time.">
Open the routine and check two things: it doesn't say **Paused**, and **When to run** shows the time you meant. A routine you just set up waits for its next time. If both look right and it still doesn't run, go through the official [checklist](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/troubleshooting#a-routine-did-not-run).
</Q>
<Q q="It says it can't read Gmail or Slack.">
Open **Connect apps** in the sidebar and find the app. If it shows **Needs auth** or **Disconnected**, choose **Authenticate**.
</Q>
<Q q="A class website wants a login.">
Save the login in <Term k="Vault" />, and the bot fills it in without seeing the password. If it isn't saved, the bot shows a Secure Form for you to fill in, and the values go straight into the page. Never paste a password into the chat.
</Q>
<Q q="The briefing is too long to read.">
Tell it what to cut, like "skip the club and talk emails," then add "keep doing this from now on, remember it." Say it outright when a preference should last, so it saves it.
</Q>
