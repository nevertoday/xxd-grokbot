Do you start every morning by going through your email, your calendar, and Slack, one after another, just to find out what your day holds? By the time you're done, a good part of the morning is gone, and the one email that mattered may still be buried under a pile of cc's. When you finish this page, your bot will post a briefing in the chat every weekday at 7:30 AM: the few things today that need you personally, each with a link to the original email or message.

What makes a briefing useful isn't how much it covers. It's whether the bot knows what "needs you" means. <mark>Spell out what "needs you" means, and the briefing stays short.</mark>

It takes four steps: give it the goal, explain how you do it now, teach it how you decide, then let it start on its own. The official docs call this a Morning briefing. See [Use cases](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/use-cases#chief-of-staff).

## The goal isn't a briefing

Writing a briefing is only one step in the process. What you actually want is to know, before you start work, which things today can't happen without you, so you can spend the rest of your time on the work itself. So make a new <Term k="bot" />, call it Potato, and give it that goal:

<Copy id="mb-goal" title="First message: the goal" code="Hi Potato. There's a goal I'd like you to own. I want to know, before I start work each day, which things today need me personally, so that nothing important slips and my mornings don't go to digging through email. This is your job from now on, so please remember it. Then, based on the goal, choose a name and a picture for yourself." />

## Walk it through your morning rounds

Now tell it how you do this today. Don't worry about saying too much. It needs to know where you look first, where you look next, and what you keep an eye out for. Here's an example. Swap in your own apps and habits:

<Copy id="mb-process" title="Second message: your process" code={`Potato, this is how I usually do this myself. Each morning I go through my email and calendar, then my Slack mentions, then what changed in my Drive docs since yesterday, and I pick out what needs me and make a list. I watch especially for calendar clashes, anything due today, and messages from my manager or customers. The apps are Gmail, Google Calendar, Slack, and Google Drive. It's right when every item links to the original email or message and matches when I open it. After I read it each day, I'll tell you what was extra and what was missing.

Think about how you could take this over from me completely. Before you begin, restate my goal and process in simple words, so I can give you feedback and confirm it. Don't start doing the work yet. Let's get the process planned first.`} />

If typing is slow, press `Cmd/Ctrl+D` and just talk, saying things as they come to you. It restates what it heard first. Correct whatever's off and go a few rounds until it's right. When you're happy, tell it to go ahead. If it needs an app, it shows a **Connect** card in the chat. Open it and sign in.

## What counts as "needs me"

When you go through your inbox yourself, you can tell at a glance which emails need an answer and which are just FYI. But you've never said those calls out loud, so the bot has no way to know them. Send one more message with the rules you go by:

<Copy id="mb-judge" title="Third message: how you decide" code={`Potato, here are a few rules I use to decide. If someone's waiting on my reply, if I have to make a decision today, or if there's a calendar clash, that counts as "needs me." Emails where I'm only cc'd count as "good to know." If yesterday's briefing already had something and nothing new has happened, leave it out. And if you're not sure whether something needs me, put it at the top and ask me. Don't drop it.

Help me find what I haven't told you about this process yet. Ask me how I make decisions, where I get my information, how I check the work, and when you should ask me for help. Ask one question at a time. Then walk me through a few examples so I can give you feedback. Don't automate anything yet.`} />

The first rule matters most, because once "needs me" has clear edges, the briefing gets short on its own. The second means you only read what's new each day. The third might look fussy, but missing one item costs more than reading one extra line, so anything unclear goes to you.

When it's done asking, try it on a real case or two that went wrong, like the customer email you almost missed. See how it would handle them, then tell it what to fix.

## Every weekday at 7:30, it shows up on its own

Once this has gone well a few times, you can let it start on its own. First have it save the process as a <Term k="技能" />, written instructions it follows every time, then set up a <Term k="routine" /> that tells it when to start:

<Copy id="mb-routine" title="Fourth message: let it start on its own" code={`Save the process we just used as a skill called "Morning briefing." Then set up a routine: every weekday at 7:30 AM, use that skill to write a briefing and post it in this chat. Don't post it to Slack for me. If you can't read one of the apps, say so at the top of the briefing. Don't fill in with old data.`} />

After the first few runs, open the routine and check that **When to run** shows the time you meant. The time zone is under **Settings**, at **Timezone**. Then open two or three links and check them against the original. If an app couldn't be read, the top of the briefing should say so.

A new routine waits for its next scheduled time. If you'd rather not wait, open it and choose **Test**, but keep in mind that a test does real work. See [Create a routine](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations#create-a-routine) in the official docs.

If you later want the briefing posted to a team channel, have it write a draft first, and send it after you've read it. For what it stops to ask you about, see [Approvals and privacy](/en/howto/approvals-privacy/).

Spend a few rounds up front on what "needs you" means, and from then on your mornings start with one short list.

## Stuck?

<Q q="The briefing didn't arrive on time.">
Open the routine and check two things: it doesn't say **Paused**, and **When to run** shows the time you meant. A routine you just set up waits for its next time. If both look right and it still doesn't run, go through the official [checklist](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/troubleshooting#a-routine-did-not-run).
</Q>
<Q q="It says it can't read Gmail or Slack.">
Open **Connect apps** in the sidebar and find the app. If it shows **Needs auth** or **Disconnected**, choose **Authenticate**.
</Q>
<Q q="The briefing is too long to read.">
Tell it what to cut, like "skip Slack small talk," then add "keep doing this from now on, remember it." Say it outright when a preference should last, so it saves it.
</Q>
