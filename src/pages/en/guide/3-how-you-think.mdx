## Say the things you do without thinking

When you explain a process, you leave a lot out, because you do those parts without thinking.

Take booking customers. You probably already know:

- **Which services take longer.** They need a bigger slot.
- **Who can do each job.** Some jobs only certain people can do.
- **When to leave extra time.** Some appointments need a gap between them.

These feel obvious to you, but the bot doesn't know them. Think back to doing the task yourself. What do you pay attention to? What makes you do something differently? Tell the bot.

## When two sources disagree, say which one wins

Information often lives in more than one place. Say an email says 3 PM and your booking system says 4 PM. Which should it trust?

<Copy id="trust" title="Swap in your own rule" code="From now on, when an email and the booking system show different appointment times, trust the booking system and tell me about that booking. Remember this rule." />

For facts that change, like appointment times, have it check the source system each time instead of its memory. The official docs agree. Memory doesn't replace the real source, so ask the bot to reopen the current data before decisions that matter. See [Memory is not a source of truth](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/bots#memory-is-not-a-source-of-truth).

## Say what done means

Sending an email doesn't finish a booking. Two more things have to be true:

- **The appointment is saved correctly.** The time and service in the system are right.
- **The customer has a confirmation.** They know when they're booked.

<mark><strong>Sending the email doesn't make it done. It's done when the booking is saved correctly and the customer has a confirmation.</strong></mark>

Each time, ask for something you can check: a link, a screenshot, or a short log of what it did.

<Copy id="done" code="When you finish each booking, tell me what you checked, and include a link to the booking record or a screenshot." />

## Have it track what's still pending

Sometimes details are missing. Say a customer hasn't replied with a day that works. Have the bot keep track of that booking, so it can pick it back up later. That way it won't forget the booking or create a second one by accident.

<Copy id="pending" code="If a booking is missing details, keep track of it and note what's missing. Before you pick it back up, check what you tracked, so you don't create a duplicate booking." />

## Test it on real examples

You don't need to explain every case up front. Pick a few real examples, ideally one where something went wrong or you had to do things differently. Ask the bot how it would handle each one, then tell it what it got right and wrong.

<Copy id="example" title="Swap in your own example" code="Here's a booking from last month: <what happened>. How would you handle it?" />

Then have it ask you:

<Copy id="ask-me" code="Potato, help me find what I haven't told you about this process yet. Ask me how I make decisions, where I get my information, how I check the work, and when you should ask me for help. Ask one question at a time. Then walk me through a few examples so I can give you feedback. Don't automate anything yet." />

## Have it remember the rules

Each decision you explained is a rule it will need every time. Ask it to remember, for example:

<Copy id="rule" code="Remember: from now on, never send messages to anyone outside without my approval." />

Details that only apply to one task can go in that message. They don't need to be remembered. See [Edit a Bot](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/bots#edit-a-bot) in the official docs.

<Callout type="tip" title="Once it works, save it as a skill">
Once it gets the whole task right with you, ask it to save the method as a <Term k="技能" />. The skill holds the steps, the decision rules, what to hand back, and what needs your OK first. Next time, the bot follows it. [Step 5](/en/guide/5-improve/) shows how.
</Callout>

## Stuck?

<Q q="It made a duplicate booking.">
<p>Tell it to keep track of bookings that are missing details. Also tell it to check its notes and the booking system before it creates a booking. Then ask it to remember that rule.</p>
</Q>
<Q q="It's following a rule that's out of date.">
<p>Ask "What do you remember about me?" to find the rule. Then correct it: "That's out of date. Now it's ..."</p>
</Q>
<Q q="There are too many cases to explain.">
<p>You don't need all of them now. Start with a few real examples. Then use the prompt above, so it asks you about the rest one question at a time.</p>
</Q>
