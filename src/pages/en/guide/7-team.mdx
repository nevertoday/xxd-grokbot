## One job per bot

Most bots work best with one clear job. A bot builds up memory for its job, so you explain it once.

Start new work with your <Term k="主 bot" />. Does the same kind of request keep coming back, with its own sources, rules or schedule? Then give it a bot of its own.

<Copy id="newbot" code="The Daily booking check keeps coming to you, and it has its own apps, rules and schedule. Make me a bot that does only this job, and name it after the job. Tell it the process and rules we agreed on. Before you create it, tell me how you'll split the work." />

Your bots can also suggest a new bot on their own. To keep your list short, have them ask you before they create one. See the official page on [your primary bot and bots with one job](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/how-grok-bot-works#your-primary-bot-and-bots-with-one-job).

## Split a bigger goal across bots

Some goals take more than one job, like a product launch or the month-end close. Ask your primary bot to set up a few bots that each own one part. They pass the work along, and you check the result.

<Copy id="team" code="I want the books closed on the 1st of every month. Set up three bots, each owning one part: one matches invoices in the finance inbox to purchase orders; one matches expense reports to receipts; one lists every mismatch for my sign-off. Tell me how you'll split it and who hands to whom, and create them once I confirm. Don't approve or pay anything." />

<mark>One job per bot, and one owner for each stage.</mark> Two bots on the same stage duplicate work and send noisy updates.

How bots hand work off:

- **Direct messages.** One bot messages another; that bot wakes up, handles it, and replies later. You can see the handoff in the conversation.
- **A group chat.** Choose **New chat** and add two to six bots in **To:**. Give each stage an owner from the start:

<Copy id="group" code="@Researcher gather the source material and link every claim. @Writer turn the findings into a launch draft. @Reviewer check the draft against the sources and list only blocking issues. Don't publish anything." />

Every bot reply counts toward your usage, including group replies and messages between bots. Fewer bots means fewer replies. These bots also share one computer and one Vault, so separate bots aren't walled off (see [step 4](/en/guide/4-apps/)).

See the official pages on [a team of bots for a bigger goal](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/use-cases#build-a-team-of-bots-for-a-bigger-goal) and [handing work off](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/chat-and-collaboration).

## Talk to your primary bot

Your primary bot is your main point of contact, the one with a star in the sidebar. It checks in with you on its own and hands work to your other bots.

Give it a request, and it can pass the work to the bot that owns that job. You don't have to remember which job belongs to whom.

- **No primary bot yet?** You'll see **Introducing your primary Bot**. Choose **Create primary Bot**, or **Choose an existing Bot** to pick one.
- **Want a different one?** Right-click your primary bot and choose **Replace with different Bot**.

## Share with teammates

There are two ways:

| | Share a template | <Term k="team bot" text="Team Bot" /> |
| --- | --- | --- |
| They get | Their own copy | The same bot as you |
| Apps and keys you added | No | Yes |

**Share a template.** Open the bot's details, choose **Share**, then **Create a copy**. The bot posts a template in the chat. Choose **Publish**, then **Copy link**. The other person opens the link and chooses **Add to Grok Bot**. They never get your computer, logins or chat history.

<u>Anyone with a public template link can open it and see the bot's name, description, skills and routines. Remove API keys, internal URLs and customer data before you share.</u>

**Team Bot.** It needs a Team or Enterprise plan and at least one other person on your team. Everyone uses the same bot, with its apps, secrets, skills and files. Each person's chat stays private. In your own chat, it shows an **Allow** card before it uses your apps. See the official page [Team Bots](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/team-bots).

## Dr Eggbot: a bot that designs bots

The author taught everything they know about automating work to <Term k="Dr Eggbot" />. It is a bot designer, and it creates high-quality bots for you. That's the article's section [Install Dr Eggbot](https://x.com/poteto/status/2123159537016533493). The official docs also suggest starting from it to set up several bots at once.

Open its [marketplace page](https://x.ai/bot/marketplace/bots/dr-eggbot-v2) and choose **Import Bot** to add your own copy.

<Copy id="eggbot" code="Dr Eggbot, I want to hand this goal to a few bots: <goal>. This is how I usually do it myself: <process>. Ask me questions one at a time first. Then tell me in simple English which bots you'd create, what each one does, and what it doesn't do. Create them once I confirm." />

<Callout type="ok">
It asks you a few questions first, then lists the bots it will create. Each one does one job.
</Callout>

Its marketplace page says each bot it makes gets one job, plus a clear list of what it doesn't do. A drafting bot, for example, doesn't send.

## Stuck?

<Q q="Several bots are doing the same thing.">
<p>Keep one owner per stage. Tell them: "Don't message other bots. Ask me first." To keep the rule, ask each bot to remember it.</p>
</Q>
<Q q="I have too many bots and the sidebar is a mess.">
<p>Use <strong>Hide from sidebar</strong>. The bot keeps its history and its routines keep running. Deleting a bot erases its conversations, memory and routines, and can't be undone.</p>
</Q>
<Q q="I don't have a Team plan. Can I give a bot to a teammate?">
<p>Yes, share a template. They get their own copy, without your computer, logins or chat history.</p>
</Q>
<Q q="I want a login to be available to one bot only.">
<p>You can't. All your bots share one computer and one Vault. Sign out of sites you no longer need, and delete sensitive files when the work is done.</p>
</Q>
