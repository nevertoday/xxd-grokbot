## Tell the goal from the process

In [the article](https://x.com/poteto/status/2123159537016533493), poteto splits automation into two parts:

- **The goal** is what you want at the end. A baked apple pie, say.
- **The process** is how you get there. Buy the ingredients, make the filling, bake, let it cool.

<mark><strong>Automation means handing the process over, so you don't do it by hand every time.</strong></mark>

Handing it over used to take a machine or code you wrote yourself. With Grok Bot, you describe the goal in everyday words and work out the process with the bot. Where the work needs code, the bot writes it.

This guide follows that order. Step 1 sets the goal. Steps 2 and 3 cover the process.

## A bot comes with a computer

A <Term k="bot" /> is an AI helper that works for you. It has a name, a job, and its own conversation.

It has a real computer in the cloud (a <Term k="云端电脑" />) with a browser, files, and a command line. Most things you can do on a computer, it can do too:

- **Browse the web.** It uses its own browser and moves its own mouse.
- **Work inside apps.** Connect an app like Gmail or Slack (a <Term k="连接的应用" />), and it works in the app directly. That is more reliable than clicking through the website.
- **Take meeting notes.** It can join a meeting and take notes for you.

The work runs in the cloud, so it keeps going after you close your laptop. See [How Grok Bot works](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/how-grok-bot-works) in the official docs.

## Your bots share that one computer

Your account has one cloud computer, and all your bots work on it.

- **Each bot gets its own screen.** Several bots can work at once, and you can watch any screen live.
- **Files and sign-ins are shared.** When one bot signs in to a site, your other bots are signed in too.

<Callout type="warn" title="Separate bots are not a wall">
Anything on the computer is available to all your bots. Sign out of a service once no bot needs it. Delete sensitive files when the work is done. See [Do my Bots share one computer?](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/faq#do-my-bots-share-one-computer) in the official FAQ.
</Callout>

## One primary bot, plus bots with one job each

Your <Term k="主 bot" /> is your main point of contact. It checks in with you on its own and hands work to your other bots.

Start new work with your primary bot. Some requests keep coming back with their own sources, rules, or schedule. Give that kind of work a bot of its own. You can also ask your primary bot to create one.

A bot with one job keeps learning that job, so you only explain it once. Step 1 starts by making a bot like that. See [Your primary Bot and Bots with one job](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/how-grok-bot-works#your-primary-bot-and-bots-with-one-job).

## What a bot remembers

Each bot has its own memory. It keeps:

- **How you like to work.** For example, short morning briefs.
- **Rules you give it.** For example, "Ask me before you send an email."
- **A work log.** What it did, with dates.

A few facts, like your name and time zone, are shared by all your bots. Tell one, and the others know.

A bot decides on its own what to keep and doesn't ask first. To make sure it keeps something, say "Remember ...". To see, fix, or remove a memory, ask in the chat:

<Copy id="memory" title="See, fix, or remove a memory" code={`What do you remember about me?
That's out of date. I moved to Denver.
Forget that I'm on the launch team.`} />

Keep these two somewhere else:

- **Numbers that change.** Prices, for example. Ask it to look them up each time.
- **Logins and cards.** These go in <Term k="Vault" />.

See [What a Bot remembers](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/bots#what-a-bot-remembers).

## Where you talk to it

- **Desktop app.** For macOS, Windows, and Linux. There is no web version.
- **Phone app.** For iPhone and Android, with the same bots and conversations as desktop.
- **X Chat.** Chat with your bots in X Chat, which is still rolling out.
- **@bot on X.** Tag @bot in a post to hand that post to your bot.
- **Slack and Microsoft Teams.** Add a <Term k="team bot" />, and your team can reach it there.

You can also talk instead of typing. Dictate a message, or start a voice chat with the bot. See [Where do I talk to Grok Bot?](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/faq#where-do-i-talk-to-grok-bot) in the official FAQ.

## Which plans include it

- **The X Subscription.** Premium, Super, and Ultra include Grok Bot. Plus doesn't.
- **Legacy Cursor plans.** Every paid individual plan and self-serve Cursor Teams include it.
- **A linked Grok account.** A SuperGrok plan or X Premium+ works once you link it.

You sign in with a Cursor account. On The X Subscription, Grok Bot shares a weekly usage pool with Grok and Cursor.

Plans change, so check [Grok Bot on The X Subscription](https://docs.x.ai/x-subscription/grok-bot) in the official docs. For more, see the how-to [Plans and usage](/en/howto/plans-usage/).

## Stuck?

<Q q="The Grok app says I've hit my limit.">
<p>Grok (the chat app) and Grok Bot are two different apps. A paywall or a <strong>Weekly SuperGrok Limit</strong> in the Grok app is not about Grok Bot.</p>
</Q>
<Q q="Can I keep two bots from seeing each other's files?">
<p>No. The computer belongs to you, and all your bots share it. Treat anything on it as visible to every bot.</p>
</Q>
<Q q="Will it touch my own computer?">
<p>It works on its own cloud computer. It runs a command on your computer only if you allow it, and by default it asks each time. The setting is under <strong>Settings → Computer → Your computer</strong>.</p>
</Q>
