The goal is handed over, so now it's time to explain how you do the work. A lot of people stall here, because they feel they have to sort every step into a neat manual before a bot can use it. You don't.

<mark><strong>Say it as it comes, have the bot restate it back to you, fill in what's wrong, and after a few rounds it's right.</strong></mark>

## Say it all in one go

Start by thinking about how you'd do the work yourself. For bookings, it might come out something like this:

> New customers usually email to book. I check which day they want and what for, then find an open slot in the booking system, put them in, and email them back to say they're booked. Oh, and before I reply, I take another look at the system to make sure the booking actually saved. After they've been in, some of them leave a review online, so I read those too…

That's enough, and it's fine if it's messy. In [the article](https://x.com/poteto/status/2123159537016533493), poteto says the best way to do this is out loud. Talk for a while, dump everything the bot needs to know for the job, and don't worry about organizing your thoughts first.

In the desktop app, choose **Start voice input** in the composer or press `Cmd/Ctrl+D`. On your phone, use **Start dictation**. When you stop, your words sit in the composer as text, so read them, fix what you want, and send. See [Dictate a message](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/chat-and-collaboration#dictate-a-message) in the official docs.

## Try to cover these six things

The author lists six things to tell the bot. The example above already touches several of them:

- The steps you usually take, what comes first and what comes next
- What you watch out for, like where things tend to go wrong
- The apps and tools you use, like your email, a spreadsheet, or a booking system
- How you check that the work was done right
- Where the information comes from, like email, Slack or Teams, a ticket, an app, or a website
- Where the feedback comes from, like customer reviews or what colleagues tell you

If you miss one or two, don't worry. When the bot restates the plan, you'll see right away what's missing.

## Have it restate the plan before it starts

Put everything you said into this message and send it:

<Copy id="process" title="Put everything you said in here" code={`Potato, this is how I usually do this myself: <your process>

I want you to think about how you could take this over from me completely. Before you begin, restate my goal and process in simple words, so I can give you feedback and confirm it. Don't start doing the work yet. Let's get the process planned first.`} />

The second half is the part that matters most. Asking it to restate shows you what it actually understood, and telling it not to start yet keeps this round to planning.

If you'd rather it follow your process exactly, put that in too. For example, "Do it exactly my way, in this order."

## Read its restatement and correct it, round by round

It's normal not to get it right the first time, and the author says not to worry about that. When the bot restates the plan, fill in whatever's wrong. If your note is about one particular reply, reply to that message and say it there. If something should hold every time, tell it "from now on, always do it this way." A few rounds of this and you'll have a process you're happy with.

Once you are, let it get started:

<Copy id="proceed" code="That looks right. Go ahead with this plan. Start by connecting the apps and tools this job needs." />

When an action needs your OK, it shows you an <Term k="审批" /> card and waits. [Step 4](/en/guide/4-apps/) covers connecting apps and signing in to sites.

## You can step in while it works

If you change your mind, just send a new message. Your message takes priority and can redirect the work in progress. If you need it to stop right away, send "Stop now," though actions it already finished are not undone. See [Redirect work in progress](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/chat-and-collaboration#redirect-work-in-progress).

## Last, have it check the process against the goal

Once the process is set, you can go one step further. Ask the bot to look back at your original goal and see whether there's a better or simpler way.

<Copy id="review" code="Check the process we settled on against my original goal. If you see a way to make it better or simpler, tell me." />

This is what [step 1](/en/guide/1-goal/) meant. Your current process is only one route, and the goal is where you're headed. Next, you'll teach the bot the calls you make from experience.
